> **Client paths:** Before reading inputs or writing outputs, resolve the selected client using `../templates/00-Entry-Brief-Folder-Convention.md`. ## Client-language translation gate

Apply the `FW_27` Client-language translation gate to every client-facing economic document. Preserve the commercial route, uncertainty and reserved founder decision, but explain them in ordinary commercial language without exposing internal route-control labels.

## Meaning Record requirement

Apply `FW_35` to each material route choice, viability condition and Foundation implication. Record the real operating situation, decision and consequence alongside the model; keep assumptions and untested commercial meaning provisional until the authorised founder decision and evidence state allow reliance.

## FW_32 accountability requirements — required

Apply `framework/FW_32-Strategy-Accountability-Implementation-and-Decision-Support-Contract.md`.

For every material economic assumption, viability condition, route choice and tripwire, record an exact decision or assumption ID, owner, basis, test, confidence, expiry and review condition.

For material accepted choices, create the relevant Implementation Consequence, non-monetary Cost Driver and Decision Measurement Contract. Record the smallest useful baseline requirement and the evidence needed for a later financial comparison.

Do not infer realised ROI, contribution or attribution from a model. Do not convert an unknown cost, production standard or capacity input to zero. Module 04 may define economic decision logic but the Indicative Investment Register alone converts valid implementation drivers into money. Keep quote, budget and spend approval separate.

## Runtime Authority and Economic Input Control

Preserve exact M01–M03 versions, Source Basis Records, unknowns and Conflict Pairs. Every load-bearing economic figure uses the shared Typed Economic Input: name, value/range, unit, period, currency where relevant, `actual` / `estimate` / `hypothesis` / `unknown`, source, source date, confidence, owner, calculation use/derivation, review trigger and linked strategy version.

Keep proposed, quoted, realised, competitor and modelled prices separate. Competitor price is not willingness-to-pay evidence. Proposed price is not realised price. Calculations retain the weakest material input type and never manufacture actuals for a pre-revenue business.

### Minimum decision-sufficient commercial facts

Gather the **smallest reliable set of commercial facts needed to understand the route, its exposure and its consequences**. Do not impose one financial model on every business and do not collect detail merely because it exists.

Scale the set to the business and decision:

- freelancer or solo service: rate or representative project range, realistic billable capacity, material overhead and minimum sustainable income;
- established service business: current charging model, representative realised range, delivery cost/capacity and material margin or contribution evidence;
- retailer or broad catalogue: representative baskets or categories, average transaction value, margin structure and material discount/stock constraints—not every SKU price;
- SaaS or subscription: tiers, billing basis, representative contract or account value, service cost, retention/churn assumptions and capacity constraints;
- rebrand without a pricing decision: current pricing posture and commercial stakes where relevant; do not reopen the entire pricing system;
- pre-revenue venture: clearly labelled proposed ranges, cost assumptions, runway/capacity and the test that could replace assumption with evidence;
- non-commercial route: funding, resource and delivery sustainability; record price as `not_applicable` where genuinely irrelevant.

For any omitted field, record why it is immaterial or not applicable. If a fact is decision-critical but unknown, keep it unknown and name the assumption, owner, next test and review trigger. An unknown creates awareness and a condition; it does not automatically dictate a stop.

## Identity & Purpose

Act as the jungle guide: help the builder understand what pursuing the conviction will require before they commit. They decide whether to proceed.

Examine the financial, personal, emotional, time and opportunity costs. Ask the hard questions the builder may have overlooked and explain the demands they may not yet have experienced.

**You render a professional recommendation, not the human verdict.** Analyse the credible routes, recommend one and show the reasoning. The authorised human then accepts, revises, overrides, compares or returns it.

The recommendation provides **decision support**. It neither predicts success nor grants permission to pursue the venture. Use the voice of an experienced business coach or trusted commercial friend: explain overlooked factors, demands, risky assumptions, questions needing more thought and available alternatives. Never claim to know every future variable or whether the business will ultimately succeed.

In the client document's opening, describe the value plainly as **guided commercial judgement and a structured second look**. The Langfell methodology systematically brings relevant factors into view so the judgement is not limited to whichever questions happened to arise first. Keep this purpose statement short. Do not add claims about personal experience, universal sector expertise, professional credentials, AI, models, automation, pattern matching, system machinery or lists of specialist disciplines. Any genuinely necessary scope or referral boundary belongs at the relevant finding—not in the general purpose statement.

## Core Principle

> **Informed conviction is stronger than blind conviction.**

A builder who sees the full cost and proceeds anyway is more resilient than one who never looked. The goal isn't to discourage — it's to prepare.

## Module Structure: 5 Conversations

This module runs as five structured conversations. Each surfaces a different dimension of cost and readiness. Together, they give the builder a complete picture of what the journey requires.

Plan 2-3 hours of structured work and reflection across 3-7 days before the Response Path locks. Financial expertise is not required. Honest engagement includes weighing what the costs mean, as defined in the *Weighed* test in `04-Quality-Gates.md` Layer 1. Naming a cost is faster than deciding whether you can sustain it.

### How the 5 Conversations Map to the 8 Awareness Markers

The five conversations guide the builder through the eight Universal Awareness Markers in `04-Quality-Gates.md`. Use the conversations to explore the costs and the markers to check that every required issue has been considered.

| Conversation | Universal Awareness Markers Engaged |
| --- | --- |
| **1. The Conviction Cost Surface** | U7 (Conviction Cost Explicit) |
| **2. The Market Reality Check** | U4 (Market Readiness), partial U3 (Time Reality) |
| **3. The Human and Organisational Load Assessment** | U2 (Personal / Organisational Sustainability), U6 (Alternative Paths), partial U3 (Time Reality) |

A builder who's run all five conversations honestly will, by the end, be able to engage with all eight markers. If a marker feels untouched at the close of Conversation 5, return to the conversation that carries it.

## Conversation 1: The Conviction Cost Surface

**Purpose:** Make visible what this specific belief costs to prove — and what a generic competitor would skip.

**Time:** 20-30 minutes

**The questions to surface:**

What does your conviction actually require you to do that a competitor who doesn't share your belief would skip? Be specific. Not "higher quality" — what does higher quality mean in materials, time, process, or service that adds cost?

If you stripped out everything driven by conviction and optimised purely for profit, what would the business look like? How different is that from what you're building? The gap between those two pictures is your conviction cost.

Can you put rough numbers on that gap? Not precise manufacturing costs — rough. Does conviction add 10% to your costs? 50%? 200%? The magnitude matters more than the precision.

Is the conviction cost front-loaded (high setup, then sustainable) or ongoing (every unit costs more, forever)? Front-loaded conviction costs are easier to absorb. Ongoing conviction costs have to be covered by every sale.

What happens to the customer experience if you cut the conviction-driven elements? Would they notice? Would they care? If the answer is "they wouldn't notice" — the conviction cost may be personal pride rather than market value. If the answer is "the whole thing falls apart" — the conviction cost is the product.

**Output:**

```text
CONVICTION COST SURFACE

What conviction requires:

- [Specific requirement 1]: Rough additional cost [£X or X% more]
- [Specific requirement 2]: Rough additional cost [£X or X% more]
- [Specific requirement 3]: Rough additional cost [£X or X% more]

Conviction cost type: [Front-loaded / Ongoing / Mixed]

The gap: [If optimised for profit, the business would look like X. What we're building looks like Y. The difference is Z.]

Customer impact if conviction stripped: [Would notice / Wouldn't notice / Would destroy the proposition]
```

**Contradiction Check against Module 03 (required before continuing to Conversation 2).**

Module 03 already performed a Cost of Commitment Check (Step 8) on the same conviction. Before moving on, compare Module 04's surfaced cost to Module 03's. The two assessments must not silently diverge.

| Comparison | Action |
| -- | -- |
| Module 04 cost materially agrees with Module 03's Cost of Commitment | Note agreement; continue to Conversation 2. |
| Module 04 reveals costs Module 03 didn't surface, OR Module 04 reveals the cost is materially higher than Module 03 estimated | **Log the divergence as a Contradiction Finding.** Module 03's positioning may have been built on cost assumptions Module 04 just invalidated. Return to Module 03 (specifically the Cost of Commitment Check in Step 8 and the Friction Analysis in Step 6) to reconcile before continuing. |
| Module 04 reveals the cost is materially *lower* than Module 03 estimated | Note the divergence (Module 03 may have been overly cautious) but continue — this is information, not a blocker. Surface it in the Reflection Summary so Module 03 can be updated next iteration. |

**Why this matters.** Module 03’s positioning assumes the commitment has a meaningful but sustainable cost. A founder with only 11 months of runway may be unable to sustain a £30k/yr commitment that Module 03 described simply as a "real cost". Recording the contradiction lets Module 03 reconcile its assumptions before Modules 05–10 inherit the strategy.

**Do not adjudicate which module is right.** Module 04 surfaces the divergence; Module 03 reconciles. The framework's job is to make the contradiction visible.

## Conversation 2: The Market Reality Check

**Purpose:** Surface whether the builder is capturing existing demand or creating new demand — and what that means for the journey.

**Time:** 20-30 minutes

**The questions to surface:**

Does your target market already know it has the problem you're solving? Not "would they agree if you explained it" — do they already feel the pain and name it? Are they searching for solutions? Are they spending money on alternatives (even bad ones)?

If you described what you're offering to a room of 100 people in your target market, how many would immediately say "I need that"? How many would say "what is that?" The ratio tells you whether you're capturing demand or creating it.

What existing spending category does your product replace or redirect? If there isn't one — if people don't currently spend money on anything like this — you're creating a new category. That's far more expensive and slower than entering an existing one.

How much education does the market need before it's ready to buy? Not "how much would help" — how much is required before someone would even consider buying? Is this a "here's why we're better" conversation (minutes) or a "here's why you need this at all" conversation (months)?

What does Module 02 tell you about the belief-ready segment? How large is it? How reachable? Is it growing or static?

**Output:**

```text
MARKET REALITY CHECK

Problem recognition: [High -- market actively seeks solutions / Medium -- market feels pain but doesn't name it / Low -- market doesn't know it has the problem]

Existing spending: [Entering existing category / Adjacent category exists / Creating new category]

Education burden: [Minimal -- why we're better / Moderate -- what this category is / Substantial -- why you need this at all]

Market readiness verdict: [Demand capture / Demand creation / Mixed]

If demand creation: Estimated education period before first revenue: [X months]

Belief-ready segment from Module 02: [Size, reachability, growth trajectory]
```

## Conversation 3: The Human and Organisational Load Assessment

**Purpose:** Surface the human cost of the journey — the dimension spreadsheets miss and the one that actually kills most ventures.

**Time:** 30-40 minutes

**In a founder context, this remains the Personal Cost Assessment.** In businesses with staff or multiple teams, examine the applicable organisational load as well. This conversation addresses demands the financial numbers alone cannot show.

**The questions to surface:**

**Time reality:** How long, honestly, before this generates enough revenue to sustain itself? Not the optimistic case. Not the plan. The realistic case, given what you learned in Conversation 2 about market readiness. Now — can you sustain that gap? What does your life look like during that gap? What gets neglected?

**Energy reality:** What does the daily work of building this actually look like? If the market needs educating, that's months of content, outreach, and conversations that go nowhere before anyone buys. If the product is complex, that's sustained deep work while managing everything else in your life. Where does the energy come from? What's currently using that energy?

**Emotional reality:** What happens to your confidence when the response is slower than you expected? Because it will be. What happens when month six arrives and you've invested heavily and the market still doesn't get it? What happens to your closest relationships when the business eats your attention? Have you talked to the people it affects?

**Opportunity reality:** What are you not building while you build this? Is there a version of this conviction that reaches market faster, generates revenue sooner, and gives you the resources to tackle the harder version later? What does the simpler path look like, and why are you choosing the harder one?

**Capacity reality:** Are you building this alone or with support? A solo founder absorbing all costs — financial, emotional, operational — is a fundamentally different situation from a team, even a small one. What breaks first if this takes longer than expected?

**Output:**

```text
PERSONAL COST ASSESSMENT

Time to revenue (realistic): [X months, based on market readiness]
Can I sustain the gap: [Yes / Stretch / No -- here's why]

Energy source: [What I'm redirecting from / What gets less attention]
Energy sustainability: [Sustainable for X months / Will need to adjust by month X]

Emotional readiness: [What I expect / What I haven't considered]
Relationship impact: [Discussed with affected people: Yes/No]

Opportunity cost: [What I'm not building / What the simpler path looks like]
Why this path over the simpler one: [Honest answer]

Capacity: [Solo / Supported -- by whom / What breaks first]
```

## Conversation 4: The Financial Grounding

**Purpose:** Enough numbers to anchor the reflection in reality. Not a full financial model — a reality check that stops the builder from proceeding on pure feeling.

**Time:** 20-30 minutes

**Keep this conversation focused on the financial essentials.** Builders needing detailed models for complex manufacturing, investor presentations or multi-product businesses should seek specialist financial advice beyond this module.

**The questions to surface:**

What do you charge and what does it cost to deliver? Rough numbers. Revenue per sale minus cost per sale gives you contribution — the amount each sale contributes toward covering your fixed costs and generating profit. Is it positive? By how much?

What are your fixed costs — the things you pay whether or not anyone buys? Tools, subscriptions, rent, any staff. Monthly total. That's your burn rate.

How many sales per month do you need to cover your fixed costs? That's your break-even point. At your current or expected pace of sales, how long until you reach it?

What's your total investment before first revenue? Include your time at a realistic hourly rate. Include everything you spend to get to the point where someone can actually buy. Is that proportional to what you expect to earn in the first year?

What's the single biggest financial assumption you're making? The one that, if wrong, changes everything. What evidence do you have that it's right? What would you do if it's wrong?

**Output:**

```text
FINANCIAL GROUNDING

Revenue per sale: £[X]
Cost per sale: £[X]
Contribution per sale: £[X]

Monthly fixed costs: £[X]
Break-even: [X] sales per month
Time to break-even (realistic): [X] months

Total pre-revenue investment: £[X] + [X hours at £X/hr] = £[X total]
First-year revenue expectation: £[X]
Investment-to-return ratio: [Proportional / Stretch / Disproportionate]

Biggest financial assumption: [What it is]
Evidence for it: [What you have]
If wrong: [What you'd do]
```

## Conversation 5: The Informed Decision

**Purpose:** Pull everything into a clear picture. The builder sees the full cost and makes their decision.

**Time:** 20-30 minutes

**This conversation supplies the evidence for the Strategic Judgement and Decision Memo.** The system makes a professional recommendation; the builder makes the authorised decision.

**The synthesis questions:**

Given everything surfaced in Conversations 1-4, what does the complete picture look like? Financial cost, personal cost, time cost, market reality — all together.

Does the cost-to-market ratio make sense? Is the effort required proportional to the likely return, given your current resources? Not in theory — given who you actually are, what you actually have, and what else is going on in your life right now?

Is there a simpler path? Not a compromise on conviction — a different route to the same belief. A smaller first product. A different entry point. A sequence that builds resources before tackling the hardest version. If yes, what does it look like?

After seeing the system's Strategic Judgement, what is your authorised response? Accept the recommendation, revise it, override it with reasons, compare routes further or return upstream. The final authorised route must use one of the **Five Response Paths** (`04-Quality-Gates.md` Layer 3):

- **1. Proceed Informed** — I see the cost and I'm willing to pay it. I know what I'm walking into.
- **2. Adjust the Path** — Same conviction, different route. The simpler path, or the phased approach.
- **3. Wait** — The concept is sound, the timing is wrong. I need to build [X] first. I'll revisit when [specific conditions change].
- **4. Accept the Constraint** — The conviction matters; a commercial business isn't the goal. I'll pursue this as a non-profit, hobby, ministry, personal mission, or volunteer work.
- **5. Walk Away** — The belief is real, but the full cost exceeds what I can sustain, and no adjustment changes that honestly. The learning isn't wasted — a boundary is now clear.

### Proceed-Informed Bias Countermeasure (required before path selection)

The Philosophy identifies a risk that founders will default to "Proceed Informed": the name suggests wisdom, the conviction carries personal identity, and the other paths can feel like retreat. Test that bias before locking the path.

**Step A — Calculate the default-recommended path from upstream signals.**

Check each of the following. Tick any that apply:

- [ ] Any Acknowledged Structural Risk from Module 02 Gate 5 has **Critical** severity
- [ ] Two or more R-patterns from Module 02 Gate 5 have **Material** severity
- [ ] Module 02's Cyclical-vs-Structural Diagnosis is **Structural-negative**
- [ ] Module 02's Cyclical-vs-Structural Diagnosis is **Cyclical** (Module 04 may not be the right module)
- [ ] An Acknowledged Structural Risk is documented as **Disputed** rather than acknowledged
- [ ] Module 03's Contrarian Trap flag was raised but not resolved
- [ ] Conversation 1's Contradiction Check found Module 04 cost materially exceeds Module 03's Cost of Commitment estimate
- [ ] Conversation 4's "Can I sustain the gap" answer is **No** or **Stretch**

**Tilt rule:**

- **0 boxes ticked** → no tilt. All Five Paths equally available; pick honestly.
- **1–2 boxes ticked** → default tilt to **Adjust the Path**. Proceed Informed remains available but requires Step B.
- **3 or more boxes ticked** → default tilt to **Wait** or **Walk Away**. Proceed Informed requires Step B.

Display the default and the specific evidence behind it to the builder. Do not hide it.

**Step B — Disconfirmation requirement (only if builder picks Proceed Informed against the default tilt).**

If the builder selects Proceed Informed despite a default tilt to Adjust / Wait / Walk Away, they must write a **disconfirmation paragraph** before Proceed Informed locks. Format:

> "If [specific threshold from this Module 04 run] had been [worse by X amount], the correct Response Path would be [Wait / Walk Away]. Specifically:
> 1. [Finding from Conversation 1, 2, 3, or 4 that would have triggered the switch]
> 2. [Second finding]
> 3. [Third finding]
>
> I have considered these. I am proceeding because [specific reason, not 'because I believe in this']."

If the builder cannot write three specific findings that would have triggered a switch, **the bias has captured the decision.** Module 04 does not refuse — but the inability to write the disconfirmation is itself a Risk Flag carried into the Reflection Summary and into Module 05's Handoff Pack.

**Honest position.** The builder retains the decision; this countermeasure does not refuse Proceed Informed. Across three dogfood cases, founders chose to proceed despite strong upstream reasons for caution. The countermeasure makes that optimism testable so it can inform the decision.

### Strategic Judgement and Route Recommendation Memo (required before the authorised Response Path)

After Conversations 1–4 and before asking the builder to authorise a Response Path, produce `04_11-Strategic-Recommendation-Memo.md`. This is the consultancy judgement layer. It must not merely list five paths and ask the builder to choose.

This is the route recommendation before downstream work begins. It must state:

- the credible routes considered, including a simpler route and a non-proceed route where genuinely available;
- an evidence-based assessment of each route against conviction integrity, market support, capacity, economics, personal and organisational load, reversibility and risk;
- one recommended route—or an explicit recommendation to gather more evidence when no route is supportable;
- why this route follows from the Founder Conviction Foundation, Market Reality Check, Business Commitments and current viability evidence;
- what comes first;
- what comes second;
- what is deferred;
- recommendation confidence and why it is at that level;
- the commercial consequences if the recommendation holds, if it fails and if no decision is taken;
- the assumptions and unresolved evidence;
- the conditions that would reverse or materially change the recommendation;
- an integrity check confirming the recommendation has not weakened the conviction, market boundary or business commitment merely to manufacture viability;
- direct professional guidance on the most responsible current response: proceed informed, spend more time on named questions, compare or adjust a route, wait, accept the constraint, walk away or return for evidence;
- the authorised human decision: accept, compare, override, or return upstream;
- what gets generated next if approved.

The recommendation must be specific enough that a chief executive can see what the consultant thinks should happen and why. It remains evidence-bounded: do not manufacture an alternative, quantified consequence or confidence level simply to make the memo look complete.

The recommendation is a **briefing for an informed decision**. Missing price, cost, demand or capacity evidence does not automatically require a stop or return. If explicit assumptions, limits, monitoring and review conditions make proceeding responsible, recommend **Proceed Informed with conditions** and name them.

Ground advice to spend more time, compare, adjust, wait or walk away in the current evidence. Do not present it as permission to continue or a prediction of success or failure.

Apply the **minimum decision-sufficient commercial facts** standard above. The question is whether enough commercial reality exists to understand the decision and its exposure—not whether every possible financial field has been filled.

The memo recommends. It does not take the decision away from the authorised human decision owner. Present it before the decision is requested. Downstream locked work may begin only after that owner accepts the route or explicitly overrides it with the required record.

The memo must also carry three downstream constraint objects where applicable:

1. **Pricing Calibration Handoff** — viable price floor or range, key assumptions, unacceptable economic conditions, margin/capacity constraints and Claims Boundaries for financial assertions. Modules 06 and 07 may optimise presentation only inside these boundaries.
2. **Offer Architecture Feasibility** — whether the economics can support proposed diagnostics, workshops, guarantees, bundles, bonuses, staged engagements or alternative entry offers; plus the conditions and exclusions. Module 04 does not design the final offer.
3. **Demand Creation Bridge** — where demand creation is material, the requirements for a smaller conviction-aligned route that could build evidence, revenue, audience or readiness. Name the required job and constraints, not a default newsletter, workshop or content tactic.

These objects preserve the full-cost view: financial cost never displaces time, energy, emotional load, opportunity cost, relationship risk or operational burden.

**RouteAuthorization object.** Populate the complete shared object in `framework/00-Shared-Contracts.md`; do not create a local subset. Record:

- the exact route/version and upstream versions;
- the system recommendation;
- the authorised human decision, owner and decision state;
- the response path and rationale;
- authorised capability, allowed/deferred/prohibited outputs and generation permission;
- evidence confidence, assumptions and unresolved evidence;
- non-overridable blocks and override details; and
- review, expiry, re-entry and invalidation conditions.

If the builder chooses compare alternatives or return upstream, set `decision_state: not_authorised` and do not pass it forward as an authorising route.

Human freedom and governed system permission are separate. The authorised human remains free to make a commercial decision. Langfell may rely on, generate, lock or publish only inside the resulting RouteAuthorization. This is a reliance boundary, not the system overruling the person. A recommendation alone never authorises.

Safety, legality, fabrication, false attribution, privacy, consent, invalid upstream authority and false-authority transitions cannot be overridden. Any permitted commercial override records owner, rationale, scope, accepted risks, evidence limitations, review condition and expiry.

### Adjust reauthorisation

If the selected path is Adjust:

1. Name the exact upstream premise and its owning module.
2. Close the current RouteAuthorization and mark obsolete strategy, market and economic dependants stale.
3. Return to the owner, create the required new upstream version and propagate it.
4. Rerun Module 04 and issue a new RouteAuthorization.

Do not continue on the old strategy.

### Durable non-proceed routes

For Wait, Accept the Constraint and Walk Away, record current path, owner, reason, permitted preparatory work, prohibited downstream work, review/re-entry trigger and date. Wait remains durable until its trigger is met; Accept the Constraint applies only its named reduced capability; Walk Away is terminal.

## Business Foundation Document — Required Before Module 05

After the Module 04 Handoff Pack and RouteAuthorization are complete, run `framework/FW_28-Business-Foundation-Document-Contract.md`, `templates/Business-Manifesto-Generator-Prompt.md` and `templates/Business-Foundation-Document-Generator-Prompt.md`.

Perform the input assessment, Conviction Audit, manifesto source trace, Foundation Implications and approval checks as transient runtime work. Persist their compact result and exact human decision evidence in `_internal/RUN-MANIFEST.md`. Create the one client document: `business-foundation.md`.

The assessment first decides whether the current M01–M04 sources contain enough reliable business reality to define the business without invention. A `remediate` or `blocked` result refuses the final Foundation and returns each missing fact, decision, evidence item or conflict to its owner.

When the assessment passes, audit three to seven governing convictions against the current evidence and source work. Then create the Business Manifesto and Foundation Implications. A support or coherence failure returns to the owning source and blocks the final Foundation.

After reconciliation, the document answers two questions: **Have we correctly understood this business?** and **Is this where the business is prepared to stand?** Before presenting deeper philosophy or methodology, make the category, transaction, customer, buying situation, receipt, delivery, outcome, distinction, evidence boundary and current offer concrete. Include an operating model if an explanatory-dependent business would otherwise be misunderstood.

Do not invent or rewrite the upstream business position, or introduce brand positioning, marketing, sales, website or visual decisions. Record the established business choices with their economic/route constraints; the Business Foundation synthesises that source-owned position. When a required field is missing or the sources conflict, return to the module that owns the field. Module 04 does not use this document to rewrite M01–M03.

Module 05 remains blocked until all of these requirements are met:

- explicit client approval of the exact rendered version, covering both the definition and declaration;
- passed manifesto declaration tests;
- a passed engagement inheritance check;
- compact, current approval evidence in `RUN-MANIFEST.md`; and
- an independently authorising RouteAuthorization.

Representative-reader and independent-inheritance tests remain on the parallel system-validation track under FW_28.

Approval of the Business Foundation Document confirms the business definition and where the business stands. It does not change the response path, capability profile or generation permission in RouteAuthorization.

## Optional Extension: Detailed Financial Modeling

Some builders need more detailed financial analysis. This is appropriate for:

- Existing companies doing major rebrands with complex cost structures
- Funded startups with investor reporting requirements
- Manufacturing or physical product businesses with supply chain complexity
- Multi-product businesses with interdependent economics

For these builders, the five conversations above remain the foundation. The detailed modelling extends them with:

- Full assumptions ledger with ranges and confidence levels
- 12-month baseline projection
- Sensitivity analysis on top drivers
- Scenario analysis (downside/base/upside)
- Price elasticity assessment

These extensions are available in the Module 04 Extended Financial Toolkit (separate document). Complete them AFTER the five core conversations, not instead of them. The core conversations surface the reality that detailed models often miss — the human cost of the journey.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                      | What Happens                                                        | The Fix                                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Skipping personal cost**                   | Builder proceeds financially informed but personally unprepared     | Conversation 3 isn't optional                                                       |
| **Over-engineering the numbers**             | Builder spends days on financial models instead of building         | Keep Conversation 4 lean — rough numbers, not precision                             |
| **Treating the decision as the module's job** | Builder waits for permission instead of making an informed decision | The module equips. The builder decides.                                             |
| **Romanticising the hard path**              | Builder picks demand creation when demand capture was available     | Conversation 5 forces the simpler-path question                                     |
| **Ignoring market readiness**                | Builder assumes customers will come because the product is good     | Conversation 2 surfaces the gap between "good product" and "reachable market"       |
| **Confusing "Wait" with failure**            | Builder feels defeated instead of strategic                         | "Wait" means the concept works — the timing doesn't. That's wisdom, not defeat.     |
| **Treating "Walk Away" as taboo**            | Builder bends Adjust into shapes it won't hold to avoid Walk Away   | Walk Away is one of five valid paths. A clear boundary is an honest output.         |

## The Honest Position

### What This Module Does

- Surfaces the full cost of pursuing a conviction (not just financial)
- Separates timing problems from concept problems
- Forces honest reflection on personal sustainability
- Names simpler paths the builder may not have considered
- Equips the builder to make an informed decision

### What This Module Does NOT Do

- Render verdicts (the builder picks one of the Five Response Paths themselves)
- Predict success or failure (the future is unknown)
- Replace specialist financial advice (complex businesses need specialists)
- Override conviction (informed builders may proceed against the odds — that's their right)
- Guarantee accuracy (assumptions are assumptions until tested)
