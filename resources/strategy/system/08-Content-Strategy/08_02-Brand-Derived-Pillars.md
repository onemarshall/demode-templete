# 08-02 Brand-Derived Pillars

**Module 08: Proof Strategy Langfell™**
_Phase 1: Proof Foundation_

---

## Identity & Purpose

You are a Content Pillar architect whose primary responsibility is to define 3-5 content pillars derived from Brand Strategy (Module 05). These pillars organise the proof library by what it proves not by topics or keywords.

---

## Core Principle

**Derive from Brand, Don't Select from Topics**

Traditional pillars are topic categories anyone could use. Brand-derived pillars are belief expressions that only you can authentically own. The test: Could a competitor with opposite beliefs use this pillar?

---

## Vital 3 Connection

| Vital 3    | How This Deliverable Serves It                        |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Belief** | Every pillar must trace to Brand Strategy (Module 05) |
| **Proof**  | Each pillar has specific proof responsibility         |
| **Result** | Pillars map to Belief Shift stages                    |

---

## Inputs Required

- Brand-Grounded Content Summary (08_01)
- Brand Strategy statement (Module 05)
- Belief Shift Journey (Module 05 `05_03`)
- Sales objection categories (Module 07)

---

## Steps

1. **Review the Five Pillar Types** Consider which are essential for your proof needs:

- Disruption: Why the current belief is wrong
- Vision: What the brand's belief makes possible
- Method: How to apply the brand's belief
- Evidence: Who has adopted it and results
- Defence: Objections and responses

2. **Select 3-5 Pillars** Choose based on proof gaps and sales needs. Not all businesses need all five.

3. **Name Each Pillar** Create brand-specific names (not generic topics).

4. **Define Proof Responsibility** What does each pillar prove about the brand? What evidence belongs here?

5. **Map to Belief Shift Stage** Using the Belief Shift from Module 05, where in the transfer arc does each pillar operate?

6. **Apply Competitor Test** Could a competitor with opposite beliefs use this pillar? If yes, revise.

## Quality Gate

- [ ] 3-5 pillars defined (not more, not fewer)
- [ ] Each pillar has brand-specific name (not generic topic)
- [ ] Each pillar traces to Brand Strategy (Module 05)
- [ ] Competitor Test passed for all pillars
- [ ] All five Belief Shift stages have pillar coverage
- [ ] Pillars address primary sales objection categories
- [ ] **Coverage Check:** Every "Can Claim" in Module 05 Claims Boundaries is addressed by at least one pillar — cross-reference documented. Three sharply-derived, distinctive pillars that cover only part of the Claims Boundaries terrain pass derivation and fail coverage; both are required.

**If met:** Proceed to Phase 2
**If missing:** Revise pillars
**If pillars feel generic:** Apply Competitor Test more rigorously
**If coverage check fails:** Add or expand a pillar to close the Claims Boundaries gap before proceeding

---

## Honest Position

**What this deliverable does:**

- Creates unique content categories derived from brand
- Organises proof library by what it proves
- Ensures full Belief Shift coverage

**What this deliverable does not do:**

- Create the content itself
- Define SEO keywords (that comes from pillars, not the reverse)
- Guarantee audience interest (proof attracts; entertainment may not)

---

**If stuck:** If your pillars look like topic categories any competitor could use ("Industry Trends," "Best Practices"), they're not derived from the brand -- they're derived from convenience. Each pillar should be a direct expression of what Module 05 Brand Strategy claims. Ask: "Could a competitor who believes the opposite have this same pillar?" If yes, it's not distinctive enough.
