# Glossary: Founder Convictions

**Module 01 DeMode™**
_Terminology Specific to This Module_

---

## Core Terms

### The Broken Thing

**What it is:** The industry practice or market condition that makes you genuinely angry.

**Why it matters:** Conviction needs a target. Without something you're working against, your belief has no direction.

**Test:** Have you felt this way for more than two years? Would you still feel it if the specific incident that triggered it hadn't happened?

---

### The Hidden Truth

**What it is:** What you know about your industry that most people don't know -- or don't see clearly.

**Why it matters:** This insight explains why the Broken Thing exists and how to fix it. That understanding gives you leverage.

**Test:** Can you point to specific experiences that taught you this? Is it something you learned through practice, not just observation?

---

### The Non-Negotiable

**What it is:** What you would refuse to do even if data showed you should.

**Why it matters:** This reveals your actual values -- not what you say you value, but what you'd sacrifice to protect.

**Test:** Has this boundary ever cost you something? Money, a relationship, an opportunity? If it's never been tested, you don't know if it's real.

---

### The Legacy

**What it is:** What you want to be remembered for creating or changing.

**Why it matters:** This connects your daily work to meaning beyond profit. It's the answer to "Why does this matter?"

**Test:** Is this legacy achievable even if no one ever knows your name? If it requires recognition to feel meaningful, it might be ego, not conviction.

---

### Core Belief

**What it is:** The single sentence that captures your conviction: "I believe that [Hidden Truth about the Broken Thing], and therefore I must [action the Legacy demands]."

**Why it matters:** Your positioning, brand identity and sales process all trace back to this statement.

**Test:** Could you explain this to a stranger at a party without losing them? Does it feel uncomfortable to say out loud?

---

## Supporting Concepts

### The Backpack Test

**What it is:** An exercise that reveals whether your conviction has history.

**How it works:** Ask "What was in my backpack at fifteen?" -- what were you doing when nobody was watching, before anyone told you it was valuable?

**Example:** Jesper Kouthoofd designed the OP-1 synthesizer from what was in his teenage backpack (four-track recorder, pedals, drum machine, synth). The product IS that backpack.

**What it reveals:** If your current work connects to what was in your backpack, your conviction predates your business decisions and has been tested through practice.

---

### Domain Mastery

**What it is:** Expertise acquired through practice, not just credentials or research.

**How to recognise it:** You know things in your body, not just your head. You understand your field at a level where you forget other people don't.

**Example:** Kouthoofd spent three years practising church organ -- not to play in churches, but to understand how musicians actually use their hands.

---

### Conviction Trace

**What it is:** The test that verifies whether any element derives from your Core Belief.

**How to use it:** Ask "Can I draw a direct line from my Core Belief to this element?" The line should be clear enough to explain in one sentence.

**Example:** Core Belief ("Expertise is earned through practice") → Plain language (not jargon) → "Because we believe expertise is earned through practice, we don't hide behind jargon that signals credentials."

---

### Competitor Test

**What it is:** A test for generic elements that do not follow from your conviction.

**How to use it:** Before inspecting the existing artefact, ask which future choice about the business's identity this element requires and which plausible alternative it rules out. Comparing competitors may reveal generic language. Disagreement alone does not establish validity.

**Example:** Both a conviction-driven gym and a dependency-driven gym can claim "We help you get stronger." Only the conviction-driven one can claim "We measure success by how little you need us."

---

### Claims Boundaries

**What it is:** The three-category system for what you can and cannot say.

| Category | Definition | How to Use |
|--|--|--|
| **Can Claim** | Fully supported by evidence | State confidently |
| **Must Qualify** | Partially supported | Soften with "We believe..." / "Designed to..." |
| **Cannot Claim** | No evidence yet | Internal only -- never use externally |

**Why it matters:** This protects you from overclaiming. It forces you to know the difference between what you believe and what you can prove.
