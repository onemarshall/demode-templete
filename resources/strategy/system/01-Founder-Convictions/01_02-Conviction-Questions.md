# Module 01: Conviction Questions

**DeMode™**
_Excavating What You Actually Believe_

---

## What This Document Does

This document serves two purposes:

1. **Preparation Guide** (Part A) — What the builder reads before the conversation. Gets them thinking about the right things without asking for polished written answers.

2. **Conversation Reference** (Part B) — The full question bank, push prompts, surface-answer indicators, and depth signals used by the AI (or human facilitator) during the guided conversation. The builder doesn't need to read Part B — it's the facilitator's reference material.

**How to use:**

If you're the builder: Read Part A. Think about the questions. Sit with them for a day or two if possible. Then enter the guided conversation (via `01-Prompt.md`). You don't need to write anything down — the conversation will draw out what's there.

If you're a practitioner facilitating for a client: Give Part A to the client as pre-work. Use Part B as your interview guide during the session.

**Input:** Domain Readiness Check complete (from `01_01-Domain-Readiness-Check.md`)
**Output:** Ready for the guided conversation (via `01-Prompt.md`)
**Time:** Pre-work thinking: 1–3 days. Conversation: 30–60 minutes.

# PART A: PREPARATION GUIDE (CF-prep)

_Read this before the conversation. Think about the questions. Don't try to produce polished answers._

> **Entry-path note:** This section is the **cold-entry prep**. If you completed Pre-Foundation (Tier 0), skip this section entirely — your handoff already surfaced this material via PF-1.x and PF-3.x. Go straight to PART B.

---

## Before We Talk

The conversation we're going to have goes deep. It works best when you've had time to think — not to prepare perfect answers, but to let your brain start chewing on the right questions.

Read these five questions. Think about them. Scribble notes if you want. Then come to the conversation ready to talk.

---

### CF-prep-1: What makes you angry about your industry?

Not mildly annoyed. Not "it could be better." The thing that's been frustrating you for years. The practice you've seen over and over that makes you think "this is wrong."

If you can rant about it for 20 minutes without notes — that's the one.

---

### CF-prep-2: What do you know that your industry is getting wrong?

From experience, not theory. The thing you keep trying to explain to people. The insight that seems obvious to you but everyone else seems to miss. The thing that, if your competitors understood it, would change how they operate.

---

### CF-prep-3: What would you refuse to do even if it cost you money?

The line you won't cross. The shortcut you won't take. The compromise that would make you not recognise yourself.

If it's never been tested — if you've never actually had to turn down the money — think about what that moment would feel like. Would you hold?

---

### CF-prep-4: What do you wish someone had told you before you started?

The hard-won truth. The thing you learned through pain that you now try to tell others. The insight that seems obvious now but took you years to see.

---

### CF-prep-5: If this works — if you succeed completely — what changes?

Not for your bank account. For the people you serve. For your industry. For the world, even in a small corner of it. What belief would people hold differently?

---

## That's It

You don't need answers. You just need to have been thinking about them. The conversation will do the rest.

Some of the best answers come after sleeping on it. Give yourself a day or two. Let the questions work on you.

When you're ready, enter the guided conversation via `01-Prompt.md`.

# PART B: CONVERSATION REFERENCE

_For the AI facilitator or human practitioner. The builder does not need to read this section._

---

## How to Use This Reference

Part B contains the full question bank for the guided conversation, organised by conviction element. For each element, you'll find:

- **Primary questions** — the main questions to ask
- **Push prompts** — what to ask when answers are surface-level
- **Surface-answer indicators** — how to recognise polish, abstraction, or borrowed language
- **Depth indicators** — how to recognise when you've reached genuine conviction
- **Contradiction handling** — what to do when answers seem to conflict

The AI conversation protocol in `01-Prompt.md` defines the flow and phases. This document is the detailed reference material for each phase.

---

## CF-1: The Broken Thing

_What's wrong with the industry/market/world that demands fixing?_

### Primary Questions

> "What practice in your industry makes you genuinely angry?"

> "What's wrong with your industry that most people seem to accept?"

> "What do competitors do that makes you lose respect for them?"

### Push Prompts (When Answers Are Surface-Level)

> "How long have you felt this way?"

> "Give me a specific example — a time you saw this and it made you angry."

> "What would people have to believe for this problem to keep existing?"

> "If you could grab your industry by the collar and say one thing, what would it be?"

> "When you see how others in your field operate, what makes you think 'This is wrong'?"

> "What complaint do you hear from customers that the industry ignores?"

> "If you could eliminate one common practice overnight, what would it be?"

### Surface-Answer Indicators

- **Generic complaints** — "Customer service is bad" (everyone says this)
- **Borrowed anger** — Something they've heard others complain about but don't viscerally feel
- **Safe criticisms** — Things that won't offend anyone if said publicly
- **Recent frustrations** — Something that annoyed them this week vs. something they've felt for years

**Test:** Have they felt this way for more than two years? Would they still feel this way if the specific incident that triggered it hadn't happened?

### Depth Indicators (You've Got It When)

- They feel heat when they talk about it
- They could rant about this for 20 minutes without notes
- They've tried to fix this before (even in small ways)
- Other people have told them to "let it go" or "that's just how it is"
- They'd feel like a fraud if they participated in the practice themselves

### Contradiction Handling

If they're angry about two things that seem to conflict (e.g. "The industry moves too fast" AND "The industry is stuck in the past"):

> "What would have to be true for both of these to make sense?"

> "Is there a deeper problem that causes both symptoms?"

> "Which one would you fight for if you had to choose?"

Don't force resolution. The tension often is the conviction.

## CF-2: The Hidden Truth

_What do you know that the industry is missing or ignoring?_

### Primary Questions

> "So if that's what's broken — what do you know that everyone else is missing?"

> "What obvious truth is everyone in your field ignoring?"

### Push Prompts

> "Who would disagree with you on this? Why are they wrong?"

> "When did you first realise this? What happened?"

> "You keep saying [word/phrase] — tell me more about what you mean by that."

> "What do you find yourself explaining over and over to clients/customers?"

> "If you could teach one thing to everyone in your industry, what would it be?"

### Surface-Answer Indicators

- **Truisms** — Things everyone would agree with ("Quality matters")
- **Contrarian for its own sake** — Disagreeing just to be different
- **Unexamined beliefs** — Things they've always assumed but never tested
- **Industry jargon** — Hiding behind language that sounds smart but says nothing

**Test:** Would a smart person in their industry push back on this? If no one would disagree, it's probably not a hidden truth — it's conventional wisdom.

### Depth Indicators

- They can explain it simply to someone outside their industry
- They have stories/evidence that demonstrate it
- Some people in their industry would disagree
- It explains why the Broken Thing keeps happening
- It suggests what to do differently

### Contradiction Handling

If they hold truths that seem to conflict ("You need to move fast" AND "You need to be patient"):

> "When is the first truth more important? When is the second?"

> "Is there a higher principle that tells you when to apply each?"

> "What mistake do people make by only seeing one side?"

These are often both true in different contexts. The higher principle is usually the real Hidden Truth.

---

## CF-3: The Non-Negotiable

_What will you do (not say) to prove this belief?_

### Primary Questions

> "What would you refuse to do even if it cost you money?"

> "What's the line you won't cross?"

### Push Prompts

> "Has this actually been tested? Tell me about a time."

> "What did it cost you?"

> "If a big client asked you to compromise on this, what would you actually say?"

### Surface-Answer Indicators

- **Cost-free commitments** — "We always put customers first" (what does that actually cost?)
- **Aspirational rather than tested** — "I would never..." without a story of when it was tested
- **No specific examples** of when it was actually hard
- **Borrowed from company values posters** — Generic principles, not lived experience

**Test:** Does this non-negotiable have real cost? If nothing is sacrificed, it's not a non-negotiable — it's a nice idea.

### Depth Indicators

- They can name a specific time it cost them something
- The cost was real (money, relationship, opportunity)
- They'd do it again
- Their team would confirm this about them
- Someone might think they're unreasonable about it

### Contradiction Handling

If they identify non-negotiables that create tension ("Honesty" AND "Kindness" — what if honesty hurts?):

> "When these conflict, which one wins?"

> "How do you navigate this tension in practice?"

> "What's the principle that tells you when to prioritise which?"

The hierarchy they reveal is the real non-negotiable.

---

## CF-4: The Legacy

_What does the world look like when this belief wins?_

### Primary Questions

> "If this works — if your belief wins — what changes?"

> "When you succeed, what will you have proven?"

### Push Prompts

> "For who specifically? Describe one person whose life is different."

> "Would you care about this legacy if no one knew your name?"

> "What would people believe differently if you succeeded?"

> "How would you know you'd won?"

### Surface-Answer Indicators

- **Grandiose without specifics** — "Change the world" with no concrete vision
- **Externally validated** — Requires fame or recognition to feel meaningful
- **Vague** — "Help people" without specifics about who or how
- **Achievable without belief adoption** — A business goal, not a legacy

**Test:** Would achieving this legacy require others to adopt your belief? If you could achieve it without anyone else changing what they think, it's a business goal, not a legacy.

### Depth Indicators

- Specific people or groups affected
- Requires belief adoption, not just behaviour change
- They'd pursue it even if unattributed
- Ambitious yet specific

## CF-5: The Hard-Won Teaching

_The truth underneath the four — what you wish you'd known before you started._

The book's Chapter 6 names this as a distinct conviction layer sitting underneath The Broken Thing, The Hidden Truth, and The Non-Negotiable: the hard-won truth the builder paid to learn through experience. It's what they'd hand forward to the person they were at the start.

### Primary Question

> "What do you wish someone had told you before you started?"

### Push Prompts

> "What did you learn the hard way that you'd save someone else from?"

> "What's the thing you'd tell yourself at the start if you could go back?"

> "If you could put one warning sign at the entrance to this work, what would it say?"

### Surface-Answer Indicators

- **Generic motivational advice** — "Believe in yourself" / "Persistence matters"
- **Things they read in business books** — Not earned, just consumed
- **The thing they'd say to inspire someone** — Performative, not the actual learned truth
- **No specific cost or moment** — If they can't name when they paid for this teaching, they probably haven't paid

**Test:** Did they pay for this teaching? If they can't name the specific instance that taught them this, it's a borrowed lesson, not a hard-won one.

### Depth Indicators

- They can name the specific moment, project, or relationship that taught them
- The teaching contradicts something they previously believed
- It's *small enough to be useful* — one specific thing, not a worldview
- Someone earlier in their career would resist the teaching
- It directly underlies The Broken Thing, The Hidden Truth, or The Non-Negotiable they've already named

### Relationship to the Other Four

The Hard-Won Teaching is often the *foundation layer* of the other four. It explains *why* the builder noticed the Broken Thing, *why* they hold the Hidden Truth, *why* the Non-Negotiable is non-negotiable. If the Teaching the builder names doesn't underlie at least one of the four, push for the deeper teaching that does.

## CF-6: Natural Language Capture

_Capturing how the builder actually talks, for Module 05 and content generators._

These questions shift from conviction excavation to language capture. Ask them after the conviction elements are established.

### Questions

> "When people ask what you do at a party, what do you say?"

> "And what do they usually say back? What do they get wrong?"

> "If you were explaining this to your mum/dad/non-industry friend, how would you describe it?"

> "What's the one thing you wish every potential customer understood before they talked to you?"

> "How do your best customers describe what you did for them — in their words, not yours?"

> "What word or phrase do you keep coming back to when you talk about this?"

### What You're Capturing

- **Natural vocabulary** — Words they keep using without thinking about it
- **High-energy phrases** — Where their voice changes, where they lean forward
- **Problem description** — How they naturally describe what's wrong (not framework language)
- **Solution description** — How they naturally describe what they offer
- **The misunderstanding gap** — What people get wrong, the "oh, like..." moment
- **Emotional core** — Why they care, beneath the rational argument
- **Customer language** — How the people they serve describe the experience

## CF-7: Synthesis and Core Belief

After all sections are complete, guide the builder to synthesise:

> "Based on everything you've told me, here's what I'm hearing. Your Core Belief is something like: [draft one sentence combining Broken Thing + Hidden Truth + Conviction Action]. Does that sound right?"

**Format:** "I believe that [Industry Default] is broken, and therefore I [Conviction Action]."

### Push Prompts for Synthesis

> "Would you defend this belief even if data, advisors, or market pressure suggested otherwise?"

> "If you had to explain this to someone in ten seconds, what would you say?"

> "Is there a simpler way to say this?"

**The one-sentence test:** If they can't say it in one sentence, they don't know it clearly enough yet. Return to the relevant section and push deeper.

## Output Processing

After the conversation, the AI uses `01-Prompt.md` to process the conversation into:

**Output A — Builder Manifesto:**
- The Broken Thing (Industry Default Belief)
- The Hidden Truth (What Competitors Miss)
- The Non-Negotiable (Proof Commitment with cost)
- The Legacy (what success proves)
- Core Belief (one sentence)

**Output B — Natural Language Bank:**
- Repeated vocabulary
- High-energy phrases
- Natural problem/solution descriptions
- The misunderstanding gap
- Emotional core
- Customer language
- First-impression intent

Both outputs are confirmed by the builder before being locked. They feed into the Evidence Matrix (`01_03-Evidence-Matrix.md`) and all downstream modules.
