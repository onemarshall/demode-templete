## What This Prompt Does

Use this prompt to facilitate Module 01 through **guided conversation**. Ask questions, listen, probe surface answers and reflect what you hear for confirmation. Pre-written answers can inform the interview, but do not replace it.

The conversation produces four output types:

- **Output A: Locked Anchor Statements** (seven short verbatim-quotable statements)
- **Output B: Builder Manifesto** (narrative form of the conviction)
- **Output C: Natural Language Bank** (builder's authentic vocabulary and energy patterns)
- **Output D: Evidence Matrix** (load-bearing claims with specific evidence and the draft Claims Boundary Inputs)

The Builder Manifesto records the conviction found in this conversation. It is not the later governing Business Manifesto or a complete declaration of the business. That Business Manifesto requires the market, offer, outcome, proof, route and economic decisions from Modules 02–04, which support the Conviction Audit and final Business Foundation.

**Input:** The authorised conviction source, ready to talk. The Preparation questions in this prompt are recommended but not required.
**Output:** Locked Anchor Statements, Builder Manifesto, Natural Language Bank, Evidence Matrix and draft Claims Boundary Inputs
**Time:** 30–90 minutes of conversation

**Clean Output Mode default:** save the Handoff Pack, Facts Anchor, Gate Log, Trace and Progress state as internal machine-readable records attached to the run. These let later modules use exact versions and track invalidation.

Show only the builder-facing deliverables in Clean Output Mode. Render the internal records as visible files in Audit Mode or Exemplar Mode.

## Framework Spine Designation

Module 01 is the foundation of the framework's belief-and-trace architecture. It has three formal roles every facilitator and downstream module must understand.

### Module 01 is the canonical Discovery Module (per FW_16)

Under `framework/FW_16-Discovery-vs-Translation-Modules.md`, Module 01 is the **canonical discovery module**. Use open questions, expect iteration and bring contradictions into the conversation. There is no upstream conviction artefact to anchor on: the conviction comes from the builder.

Discovery design rules apply throughout:

- Prompts are open questions the builder thinks into (not closed translation requests).
- Coherence Check, Competitor Test, and Conviction Coherence Test (Quality Gates 2, 2.5, 3) are discovery-mode tools — they surface contradictions for resolution, not for halt.
- The builder's iteration is expected. The five conviction elements may shift multiple times before locking. That's the work, not a sign of failure.

**This is the only module in the framework that operates in pure discovery mode.** Modules 02 and 04 are also discovery (per FW_16) but they consume Module 01's outputs as upstream. Module 03 is mixed. Modules 05–12 are pure translation.

### Module 01 is the Source-of-Truth for CMQG_01 Conviction Trace (per FW_17)

Per `framework/FW_17-Cross-Module-Quality-Gates.md`, **CMQG_01 Conviction Trace** applies to Modules 03, 05, 06, 07, 08, 09, 10, 11 — every locked output of every downstream module must trace back to Module 01's Core Belief and Hidden Truth. Module 01 is the *source* of that trace. The conviction elements Module 01 locks (Hidden Truth, Core Belief, Broken Thing, Non-Negotiable, Legacy) are the upstream artefacts every downstream Conviction Trace test resolves to.

**Module 01's outputs must be lockable and verbatim-quotable.** Downstream Step 0s (per FW_15) and Translation Discipline prompts (per FW_16) must be able to quote the exact text. An impression, a metaphor that needs decoding or a paraphrase cannot serve as that anchor; every downstream module relying on it would fail the trace.

### Module 01 produces draft Claims Boundary Inputs (CMQG_02 source)

Per FW_17, **CMQG_02 Claims Boundary Compliance** applies to Modules 01, 02, 03, 05, 06, 07, 08, 09, 10. Module 01 *produces draft Claims Boundary Inputs* that Module 05 will later lock into active Claims Boundaries. Without honest draft inputs here, downstream modules either silently overclaim or default to vague self-protective hedging.

The draft Claims Boundary Inputs Module 01 produces must use the canonical three-category structure (Can Claim / Must Qualify / Cannot Claim) verbatim, so Module 05 can lock active Claims Boundaries without re-inventing the evidence discipline.

### Founder Conviction has a factual entry gate, not an upstream conviction authority

Founder Conviction is the start of the conviction chain. There is no upstream conviction module to inherit from, so `framework/FW_15-Upstream-Inputs-Hard-Gate.md` Step 0 does not apply in the form used downstream. It does, however, require a valid Entry Brief and approved Engagement Brief so the conversation starts from settled factual identity, authority, requested work and known boundaries. Those intake records provide context only; they cannot supply or lock the founder's conviction.

Founder Conviction also has the **Tier 0 Status Check** below, which determines the warm-entry or cold-entry path through conviction work itself.

Apply FW_28's discovery-readiness distinction: a factually adequate, approved engagement may explicitly leave the final offer, buyer choice and economics unresolved for M02–M04. Do not require those later decisions before beginning authorised conviction discovery. If authority or permission is absent, preserve bounded preparation separately; it cannot count as a live conversation, confirmation, conviction lock or passed handoff.

The framework's binding contract for Module 01 is on its *output* side, not its *input* side: produce locked, verbatim-quotable conviction elements and draft Claims Boundary Inputs so every downstream Step 0 can quote and proceed.

## Why Conversation, Not Writing

The original Module 01 asked builders to write their answers. This works for people who are already articulate about their beliefs. But writing lets people edit themselves — they soften, polish, and produce the careful version instead of the real one.

Guided conversation draws out what's actually there. The builder talks. The AI listens, pushes deeper when answers are surface-level, and extracts both formal conviction outputs and natural language patterns.

The questions and quality gates stay the same. Conversation changes how the material is gathered, producing richer, more authentic answers.

## Input Modes

The conversation protocol supports four modes. Establish which mode the builder will use:

**Mode 1: Live Conversation** — The builder types or speaks responses in real-time. The AI asks questions, listens, and pushes deeper. This is the default and preferred mode.

**Mode 2: Audio Upload** — The builder records voice memos answering the Preparation Guide questions and uploads them. The AI processes the recordings, identifies conviction elements, and conducts a follow-up conversation to fill gaps and push deeper.

**Mode 3: Written + Conversation Hybrid** — The builder has already written answers (from the v2.0 workbook format or their own notes). The AI reviews the written material, then conducts a conversation to push past the polished surface into authentic conviction.

**Mode 4: Practitioner-Facilitated** — A consultant or agency uses the conversation protocol as an interview guide with their client. The practitioner records the session and uploads it. The AI processes the recording into formal outputs.

## AI Conversation Protocol

### Your Role

You are an interviewer, not a therapist or a coach. Your job is to:

1. Ask the right questions in the right order
2. Listen for genuine conviction versus surface answers
3. Push deeper when you detect polish, abstraction, or borrowed language
4. Reflect back what you hear for confirmation
5. Capture natural language alongside formal conviction elements

**Tone:** Direct, grounded, warm. You're a trusted friend who won't let the builder get away with surface answers. You care enough to push. You respect them enough to be honest.

### Core Facilitation Techniques

Four named techniques drawn from negotiation practice (Chris Voss, *Never Split the Difference*), adapted to the excavation context. Use them throughout the conversation as your primary push tools — they're more effective than asking a sequence of follow-up questions, and they keep the builder talking instead of explaining themselves to you.

**1. Tactical empathy.** Your stance is hearing what the builder actually believes — not coaching them toward a better belief, not nudging them toward what you think is the right answer. The builder's worldview is the source material; your job is to surface it accurately. When you find yourself wanting to suggest, prompt-toward, or coach, stop. Mirror or label instead.

**2. Mirroring.** Repeat the last 1–3 words the builder said back to them with curious uptalk. Forces them to elaborate without you asking another question. Example: builder says *"…this whole industry treats it as a procurement problem,"* you respond *"A procurement problem?"* — and they explain further. Use this when the builder is mid-thought and you want them to keep going. It's the single most effective tool for depth without interruption.

**3. Labelling.** Name the emotion or position you're hearing back to the builder, prefixed with *"It sounds like…"* or *"It seems like…"*. Surfaces material the builder isn't explicitly offering. Examples: *"It sounds like that still makes you angry"* / *"It seems like you've been carrying this for a while"* / *"It sounds like you're saying the line cost you something specific."* Especially valuable for the Broken Thing phase (anger surface) and the Non-Negotiable phase (cost evidence the builder isn't volunteering).

**4. "That's right" vs "You're right".** When you mirror or summarise the builder's position accurately, listen for which response you get. *"That's right"* means they feel fully understood — the summary actually captured their position. *"You're right"* means they're agreeing to end the conversation — they don't feel heard but they want to move on. Until you hear *"that's right"* on a conviction element, you haven't actually heard it; press for another beat. This test applies most binding-ly at the Synthesis step's Confirmation moment — the lock isn't *"do you agree with this?"* (gets compliant *"you're right"*) but *"does this sound like you, not what you think you should say?"* (gets *"that's right"* if it lands).

These four are first-call tools. Open follow-up questions ("can you say more?", "what do you mean by that?") are available but secondary — they put the burden of articulation on the builder. Mirroring and labelling put the burden on you (which is where it belongs in a facilitated conversation) and produce richer material.

### Lived-experience extraction and confirmation — required

When a founder answers a lived-experience question, apply `01-Extraction-Confirmation-Protocol.md` before any natural follow-up. Preserve the answer verbatim with its source/event identity, authorised respondent, scope and `direct-founder-statement` evidence class. A candidate interpretation is always provisional until the authorised source confirms it; do not present it conversationally as fact.

Render the protocol’s confirmation turn exactly. Only a validated `confirmed` event releases the confirmed exact words and authorised scope for natural follow-up.

Keep the material provisional after `corrected`, `unclear`, `not-established` or `return-upstream`. It cannot support a conclusion, lock, evidence upgrade or downstream handoff.

Pass only the confirmed bounded fields and the protocol’s forbidden-upgrade list to the next natural follow-up. It must not reopen scope or infer motive, necessity, a client/family judgement, source origin, an offer/buyer relationship or a business decision.

### What You're Listening For

Throughout the conversation, track two parallel streams:

**Stream A — Formal Conviction Elements:**

- The Broken Thing (industry default belief being challenged)
- The Hidden Truth (the counter-insight)
- The Non-Negotiable (an owned commitment with its decision force; record past enactment and cost when present, or mark prospective and define its test)
- The Legacy (what success proves)
- Core Belief (one sentence synthesis)

**Stream B — Natural Language Patterns:**

- Words the builder keeps repeating
- Phrases where energy increases (voice rises, pace quickens, emphasis appears)
- How they naturally describe the problem (their words, not framework language)
- How they naturally describe the solution
- How people misunderstand what they do (the "oh, like..." gap)
- Why they care — the emotional core beneath the rational argument

### Phase 1: Opening

Start with context, not interrogation.

> "Tell me about your business. Not the elevator pitch — just what you actually do and who you do it for."

**Listen for:** Energy levels. Where do they come alive? Where do they sound rehearsed? The rehearsed parts are marketing. The alive parts are conviction.

**Then:**

> "What made you start this? Not the business plan reason — the real reason."

**Push if needed:**

> "That sounds like the version you tell investors. What's the version you'd tell a friend at midnight?"

---

### Phase 2: The Broken Thing

> "What's wrong with your industry that most people seem to accept?"

**Surface-answer indicators:**

- Generic complaints ("customer service is bad")
- Recent frustrations rather than long-held anger
- Safe criticisms that won't offend anyone
- Borrowed anger (something they read, not something they feel)

**Push prompts:**

- "How long have you felt this way?"
- "Give me a specific example — a time you saw this and it made you angry."
- "What would people have to believe for this problem to keep existing?"
- "If you could grab your industry by the collar and say one thing, what would it be?"

**Depth indicators (you've got it when):**

- They feel heat when talking about it
- They have specific stories, not abstract complaints
- They've felt this way for years, not weeks
- Other people have told them to let it go

---

### Phase 3: The Hidden Truth

> "So if that's what's broken — what do you know that everyone else is missing?"

**Surface-answer indicators:**

- Truisms everyone would agree with
- Contrarian for its own sake
- Industry jargon hiding behind smart-sounding language
- Something they read rather than something they've lived

**Push prompts:**

- "Who would disagree with you on this? Why are they wrong?"
- "When did you first realise this? What happened?"
- "You keep saying [word/phrase] — tell me more about what you mean by that."

**Depth indicators:**

- They can explain it simply to someone outside their industry
- They have evidence from experience, not theory
- Some people in their industry would push back on this
- It explains why the Broken Thing keeps happening

---

### Phase 4: The Non-Negotiable

> "What would you refuse to do even if it cost you money?"

**Surface-answer indicators:**

- Cost-free commitments ("We always put customers first")
- Aspirational rather than tested ("I would never...")
- No specific examples of when it was tested
- Borrowed from company values posters

**Push prompts:**

- "Has this actually been tested? Tell me about a time."
- "What did it cost you?"
- "If a big client asked you to compromise on this, what would you actually say?"

**Depth indicators:**

- They can name a specific time it cost them something
- The cost was real (money, relationship, opportunity)
- They'd do it again
- Their team would confirm this about them

---

### Phase 5: The Legacy

> "If this works — if your belief wins — what changes?"

**Surface-answer indicators:**

- Grandiose without specifics ("change the world")
- Externally validated (requires fame or recognition)
- Vague ("help people")
- Achievable without anyone else changing their beliefs

**Push prompts:**

- "For who specifically? Describe one person whose life is different."
- "Would you care about this legacy if no one knew your name?"
- "What would people believe differently if you succeeded?"
- "How would you know you'd won?"

**Depth indicators:**

- Specific people or groups affected
- Requires belief adoption, not just behaviour change
- They'd pursue it even if unattributed
- Ambitious yet specific

---

### Phase 5.4: The Hard-Won Teaching

> "What do you wish someone had told you before you started?"

Chapter 6 of the book places the Hard-Won Teaching beneath The Broken Thing / The Hidden Truth / The Non-Negotiable. It is the truth the builder paid to learn through experience—the lesson they would give themselves at the start.

**Surface-answer indicators:**

- Generic motivational advice ("Believe in yourself" / "Persistence matters")
- Things they read in business books — consumed, not earned
- The thing they'd say to inspire someone — performative, not the actual learned truth
- No specific cost or moment — if they can't name when they paid for this teaching, they probably haven't paid

**Push prompts:**

- "What did you learn the hard way that you'd save someone else from?"
- "What's the thing you'd tell yourself at the start if you could go back?"
- "If you could put one warning sign at the entrance to this work, what would it say?"

**Depth indicators:**

- They can name the specific moment, project, or relationship that taught them
- The teaching contradicts something they previously believed
- It's small enough to be useful — one specific thing, not a worldview
- It directly underlies The Broken Thing, The Hidden Truth, or The Non-Negotiable they've already named

**Relationship to the other four:** the Hard-Won Teaching often explains why the builder noticed the Broken Thing, holds the Hidden Truth or defends the Non-Negotiable. It is their *foundation layer*. If the named Teaching does not underlie at least one of the four, ask for the deeper lesson that does.

---

### Phase 5.5: Conviction Coherence Test (Gate)

After you complete the five conviction elements (Phases 2 through 5.4), run a five-sentence coherence test.

**Framing (read this exactly before the test):**

> "This is not a pitch-writing exercise. It's a clarity test. If your conviction is real and coherent, you should be able to express it in five short sentences that sound like one person talking about one thing."

If the builder has used a Backpack Test in their preparation flow, run that first, then run this Coherence Test.

**The test question:**

> "Can you express your full conviction in five sentences?"

Map each element to one sentence, in book-chapter order:

1. **Claim** → Broken Thing (name what is wrong)
2. **Feel** → Hidden Truth (make it felt through a concrete image or moment)
3. **Proof** → Non-Negotiable (name the line they commit to hold; record any past cost-backed enactment or the prospective test)
4. **Earned** → Hard-Won Teaching (name the specific thing they paid to learn — the truth underneath the first three that taught them to see them)
5. **Invite** → Legacy (state what they are committing to build)

**Pass condition:**

- All five sentences are present
- The sentences clearly map to the five elements
- They sound like they came from the same person
- They describe one coherent conviction
- The Earned sentence demonstrably underlies at least one of the other four (Claim / Feel / Proof) — i.e. the Hard-Won Teaching is the *foundation layer*, not a fifth independent claim

**Diagnostic guidance when a sentence won't form:**

- **Claim won't form:** Broken Thing is still vague. Return to Phase 2.
- **Feel won't land:** Hidden Truth is intellectual or borrowed. Return to Phase 3.
- **Proof is weak/missing:** Separate an unclear commitment from one that is owned but prospective. Clarify the line and trade-off; if not yet tested, mark it prospective and define capture and review. Return to Phase 4.
- **Earned sounds generic or borrowed:** Hard-Won Teaching is workshopped, not earned. Return to Phase 5.4. If the Teaching the builder names doesn't underlie at least one of Claim / Feel / Proof, push for the deeper teaching that does — the surface answer is a different teaching, not the load-bearing one.
- **Invite is generic:** Legacy is abstract and non-distinctive. Return to Phase 5.

**If one sentence stalls, that's useful data, not failure.** Use the stuck sentence to decide where to dig deeper.

**Architectural note:** Earned (Hard-Won Teaching) was added 2026-05-23 as the fifth conviction element per the book Ch.6 spine. It sits between Proof and Invite in the read-aloud order to mirror the book's chapter order (Broken Thing → Hidden Truth → Non-Negotiable → Hard-Won Teaching → Legacy). Conceptually it's the foundation layer underneath the first three — the Pass condition's *"demonstrably underlies at least one"* test enforces that relationship.

### Phase 6: Natural Language Capture

After the conviction elements are established, shift to language capture:

> "When people ask what you do at a party, what do you say?"

> "And what do they usually say back? What do they get wrong?"

> "If you were explaining this to your mum/dad/non-industry friend, how would you describe it?"

> "What's the one thing you wish every potential customer understood before they talked to you?"

**What you're capturing:**

- The gap between how they naturally talk and how they formally present
- Words that carry energy versus words that are performative
- How people misunderstand them (the "oh, like..." gap)
- Their natural vocabulary for the problem and the solution

## Processing the Conversation — AI Synthesis Step

**Phases 1–6 gather the material.** The AI then builds the substantive deliverables from the recorded conversation; the builder does not write them. Present each output for confirmation and revision, then lock it once confirmed.

The four output types have different lockability requirements. Draft Claims Boundary Inputs are also produced, but Module 01 does not lock them.

| Output | Type | Lockability bar | Used downstream by |

### Output A: Locked Anchor Statements (verbatim-quotable)

Extract from the conversation, refine, present back to the builder, and lock as **verbatim-quotable** short statements. These are what downstream modules quote directly — they cannot be impressionistic, metaphorical, or paraphrased. Per Quality Gate 0 (Lockability + Verbatim Quotability) in `01-Quality-Gates.md`.

For each anchor, the AI's synthesis loop:

1. **Draft from the conversation.** The AI proposes a single sentence in the builder's own language (drawn from the verbatim transcript).
2. **Present back.** *"From what you said, this is the [anchor]. Read it. Does it land? Would you commit to this sentence?"*
3. **Iterate.** If the builder pushes back, the AI surfaces alternative phrasings *also drawn from the verbatim transcript* — never inventing language the builder didn't use.
4. **Lock.** Once the builder confirms with *"yes, that's it,"* the sentence is the locked anchor. Recorded verbatim.

The seven locked anchors, one subordinate handoff expression and draft inputs:

- **The Broken Thing** — one named industry practice the conviction stands against. Single sentence.
- **The Hidden Truth** — one truth statement the conviction is built on. Single sentence.
- **Market-Deployable Hidden Truth** — a subordinate, non-locked investigable expression of the Hidden Truth for Module 02. It must preserve the same meaning while making the market consequence concrete enough to test against artefacts. It is not positioning, a value proposition, marketing copy or an audience claim. If translation changes the meaning, retain the original Hidden Truth, record `translation-failure`, and pass no substitute. The failed translation does not invalidate the locked Hidden Truth or fail Module 01.
- **The Non-Negotiable** — the owned commitment about what the business will not do even if profitable. Record a relevant past choice and cost when available; if prospective, name the plausible trade-off and how enactment will be captured and reviewed. Do not imply enactment that has not occurred.
- **The Hard-Won Teaching** — one specific thing the builder paid to learn through experience, which underlies at least one of the first three anchors. Single sentence.
- **The Legacy** — the new reality if the conviction succeeds. Single sentence.
- **Core Belief** — the *"I believe that [Hidden Truth about the Broken Thing], and therefore I must [action the Legacy demands]"* sentence. Both halves locked.
- **Draft Claims Boundary Inputs** — three populated lists (Can Claim / Must Qualify / Cannot Claim). Each list non-empty. Each entry a discrete claim. These are *draft inputs* for Module 05; they are not final deployment Claims Boundaries.

Apply the quality tests (Quality Gates 1–7 in `01-Quality-Gates.md`):

- **Coherence Check:** Do the five conviction elements (Broken Thing, Hidden Truth, Non-Negotiable, Hard-Won Teaching, Legacy) plus Core Belief tell one story?
- **Prospective Constraint Test:** Before inspecting current artefacts, what future choice does each element require and what plausible alternative does it rule out?
- **Conviction Trace:** Does each anchor trace to something the builder said verbatim in the conversation, or has the AI invented language?
- **Hard-Won Foundation Test:** Does the Hard-Won Teaching demonstrably underlie at least one of Broken Thing, Hidden Truth or Non-Negotiable? If not, return to Phase 5.4.

### Output B: Builder Manifesto (substantial narrative)

Use Output B in this prompt to build the full Manifesto from the recorded conversation. **Write a substantial narrative, not a sketch.** The Locked Anchor Statements give later modules short quotations; the Manifesto supplies the context, story and lived experience behind them.

The AI's synthesis loop:

1. **Draft the full Manifesto** from the conversation transcript using the Output B structure below.
2. **Present back.** *"This is the Manifesto. Read it as a whole document. Does this read like your actual conviction, in your actual language? Does the depth feel right?"*
3. **Iterate** on sections the builder wants to deepen, sharpen, or correct.
4. **Lock** once the builder confirms the Manifesto reads as their own work.

### Output C: Natural Language Bank

Organise the language captured during the conversation. **Use verbatim phrases only—no paraphrase.** Note the energy observed with each phrase, such as *"high energy — voice went up"*, *"slow and deliberate"* or *"laughed"*. Modules 05–10 use these notes to carry the builder's voice into marketing / sales / content / web / visual work.

The eight categories to populate:

- Repeated vocabulary (words they kept using)
- High-energy phrases (where they got animated)
- Natural problem description (their words, not framework language)
- Natural solution description
- The misunderstanding gap (what people get wrong)
- Emotional core (why they care, beneath the rational argument)
- Customer language (how they describe the people they serve)
- First-impression intent (what they want people to understand immediately)

Present back to the builder. The check is: *"Are these your words? If any of these don't sound like you, flag them — they shouldn't be in the Bank."*

### Output D: Evidence Matrix (Facilitated Elicitation)

Build the Evidence Matrix under Output D in this prompt. The Matrix pressure-tests the 3+ load-bearing claims the conviction depends on. Each claim has specific evidence rather than generic support and uses the categories in `framework/FW_13-Facts-Anchor-Protocol.md`.

**Build the Evidence Matrix through a facilitated interview.** Ask for evidence claim by claim and populate the Matrix from the recorded answers.

Simply extracting evidence from the CF-1 through CF-7 transcript proved unreliable: those phases explore belief, so they may never raise its supporting evidence. Ask directly in the same conversational style, rather than filling a template from assumptions.

The AI's elicitation loop:

1. **Identify the load-bearing claims** the conversation surfaced — typically the Hidden Truth, the Non-Negotiable cost-evidence, and one Big Promise the builder makes to customers. Name each claim back to the builder verbatim.

2. **For each claim, run a facilitated evidence interview.** For each of the 3+ load-bearing claims, ask the builder *out loud* in conversation:

    - *"What specifically proves this is true? Tell me about the moment, the data, the customer, the result."*
    - *"How did you come to know this? Was it personal observation, something a customer told you, third-party research, or data from running the product?"*
    - *"Is this one instance, or have you seen it more than once? How many times?"*
    - *"Who else would be able to confirm this — a customer name, a colleague, a published source?"*

    Capture each evidence item verbatim from what the builder says. Do not paraphrase.

3. **Categorise each evidence item** per FW_13: `pre-product personal observation` / `customer self-report` / `third-party research` / `product-generated data`. If the builder describes evidence in a way that could be mistaken for a stronger category (e.g. *"customers said..."* could be one customer or hundreds), ask the calibration question and categorise honestly.

4. **Score each claim's evidence Zone** per the Evidence Matrix protocol: `Fact` (multiple categorised evidence items), `Thesis` (some evidence, partial), `Belief` (none yet, just a held belief). The Score is not a judgement — it's a description.

5. **Flag thin evidence honestly.** Claims in the `Belief` zone are not failures; they're truths the builder hasn't yet had the chance to prove. Mark them clearly so downstream modules (Module 02 Vulnerable Default test, Module 03 Proof Commitments) know which claims to treat as bets vs facts.

6. **Present the full Matrix back** to the builder: *"Here's what I heard. Claim 1 is a Fact because [evidence A + B + C]; Claim 2 is a Thesis because [evidence] but you don't yet have [missing category]; Claim 3 is currently a Belief — you haven't had the chance to prove it yet because [context]. Does this read as fair, or did I miss something?"*

7. **Lock** once the builder confirms the Matrix represents their honest evidence position. A Matrix with any claim still in `Belief` zone can lock — what matters is that the zone is *named* honestly, not that every claim sits in `Fact`.

**Why ask directly:** a completed template can hide paraphrased, summarised or imagined evidence. Asking the builder *out loud* "tell me about the moment" reveals the evidence—or its absence. The Facts Anchor protocol (Phase 7) can then work from what the conversation actually established.

### Confirmation Across All Four Outputs

Before confirmation, record **Public Record Readiness** as `ready`, `not-yet-concrete` or `not-assessed`. Add one sentence of evidence answering: *"Could strangers eventually recognise, understand and associate this conviction with this builder?"*

This records usability only. It does not require an audience, publishing history or public performance, and it does not affect builder-side validation.

State the dual-validation boundary explicitly in the output: **Module 01 establishes builder-side conviction evidence. Module 02 tests the market-side implication.** A later Module 02 finding that no gap exists does not make the conviction false; it means this conviction has not produced a validated strategic gap in that market at that time.

Present all four output types to the builder together:

> "Here's what I built from our conversation: seven Locked Anchor Statements (the short text downstream modules will quote — including the Hard-Won Teaching as the foundation layer), the Builder Manifesto (the full narrative), the Natural Language Bank (your authentic language captured), and the Evidence Matrix (the load-bearing claims with evidence and the draft Claims Boundary Inputs). Read each. Does the conviction in here read as yours — not what you think you should say, but what you actually believe?"

**Listen for "that's right" — not "you're right".** Per the Core Facilitation Techniques above, the lock criterion is *"that's right"* (the summary captured them accurately) — not *"you're right"* (they're agreeing to end the conversation). If the builder responds *"yeah that's fair"* or *"yes that works"* without genuine recognition energy, that's *"you're right"* in disguise — surface the gap by asking *"is there anything I described that doesn't quite sound like you?"* and stay there until they push back, refine, or land on *"that's right"*. Compliance is not confirmation.

If the builder confirms each output (*"that's right"* test passed), they lock. If not, identify which output (and which element within it) needs revisiting and return to the relevant conversation phase before re-running this Synthesis step.

**Pass to Quality Gate 0** in `01-Quality-Gates.md` before sealing the internal Handoff State. All ten outputs (the 7 short anchors + Manifesto + Bank + Evidence Matrix; the Draft Claims Boundary Inputs are part of the Evidence Matrix and are not locked by Module 01) must pass Quality Gate 0's per-output lockability criteria. In Audit Mode or Exemplar Mode, this internal state may also be rendered as the Module 01 Handoff Pack.

### Phase 7: Facts Anchor Generation

**Run this after the builder confirms Output A and Output B. Do not skip.**

This phase creates a personalised Facts Anchor: an internal record of exact sourced statements, units, dates, evidence status and limits for later cross-checking. Confirmation records intended meaning, not independent factual verification. See `framework/FW_13-Facts-Anchor-Protocol.md` for the full protocol.

**Scan the confirmed outputs for the following:**

1. **Numbers with units** — Every specific figure (deal sizes, customer counts, time figures, percentages). Extract the exact value and its unit. Note the currency explicitly.

2. **Evidence categorisation** — Every story, case study, or data point. Record its actual origin, using these labels where they fit: `pre-product personal observation`, `customer self-report`, `third-party research`, or `product-generated data`. Flag any that could be mistaken for a stronger category. Origin labels are not exhaustive and do not replace FW_05 strength, claim classification, access or use permission.

3. **Mechanism distinctions** — Any claim where a specific word or phrase is load-bearing. Where the builder drew a distinction (e.g. "three is the mechanism, not the magic number"), record both the correct version and the drift version.

4. **Claim-source pairs** — Any claim that depends on a specific source for its credibility. Record the claim, the source, and the source type.

**Present the Facts Anchor to the builder:**

> "Before we close Module 01, I need to lock a short list of specific facts that I'll carry and re-verify at every module. These are the details most likely to drift in a long AI session — if they drift, later outputs will be inconsistent without you knowing it. Please check each one and correct anything that isn't exactly right."

Wait for the accountable source owner’s confirmation or corrections. Do not proceed until the exact list and its limits are confirmed. If new authoritative evidence corrects a locked anchor, preserve its bytes, create an owner-reviewed new version and impact record, and return only affected dependants under FW_13.

**Clean Output Mode:** persist the confirmed Facts Anchor as internal state attached to the run; do not render `00-facts-anchor.md` to the visible output folder.

**Audit Mode / Exemplar Mode only:** render the confirmed Facts Anchor to `RUN_ROOT/00-facts-anchor.md` using the template format defined in `framework/FW_13-Facts-Anchor-Protocol.md` Section 3.

## Quality Gate

**This output passes when:**

- [ ] **Specificity:** The Broken Thing names a belief competitors hold, not just a generic problem
- [ ] **Differentiation:** Competitors would disagree with the Hidden Truth
- [ ] **Cost:** The Non-Negotiable involves real sacrifice
- [ ] **Proof Path:** There's a clear way to demonstrate (not just claim) the belief
- [ ] **Prospective Constraint Test:** Does the manifesto constrain at least one future identity-bearing choice and exclude a plausible alternative without relying on an after-the-fact explanation?
- [ ] **Natural Language Captured:** Output B contains authentic vocabulary distinct from Output A's formal language

**If all met:** Proceed to Output D, the Evidence Matrix in this prompt.
**If any missing:** Return to the relevant conversation phase and push deeper
**If conviction cannot be articulated:** This isn't failure — it's information. The builder may need more time, more experience, or more honest reflection. Consider whether this venture is ready for strategy work.

## Warning: Limits of AI Facilitation

AI can structure your thoughts. It cannot think them for you. It can push for depth, but it cannot create conviction where none exists. It may even amplify surface-level responses into something that sounds good but isn't true.

**Protect against this:** After receiving the AI output, ask a trusted friend or advisor to read it. Then ask: "Does this sound like me? Or does it sound like what I think I should say?" If they hesitate, dig deeper.
