# Langfell Manifesto — Conviction to Declaration Prompt

> **Client paths:** Before reading inputs or writing outputs, resolve the selected client using `00-Entry-Brief-Folder-Convention.md`. Its Input, Output, collection, asset and private run destinations override legacy flat output examples. Keep existing authority and approval gates unchanged.


## Purpose

Create the **Business Manifesto** as the declaration layer between conviction excavation and final Business Foundation formalisation.

The manifesto is not a summary of the strategy. It is the moment the business takes a stand.

It turns the beliefs, decisions, principles and tensions established in the assessed source work into a clear declaration of:

> This is what we believe.  
> This is what we reject.  
> This is what we stand for.  
> This is what we intend to change.  
> This is how we choose to operate.  
> This is the future we are committed to building.

The manifesto captures the living heart of the business before that thinking is formalised into the complete Foundation. It is where analysis becomes commitment.

## Governing authority and sequence

Run this prompt after the Business Definition Input Assessment has passed and the relevant M01–M04 and permitted adopted commercial evidence is sufficient to test the governing convictions. Run it before the final Business Foundation is formalised and before the founder approves the exact Foundation version.

Use this sequence:

```text
evidence
→ conviction excavation
→ identity-level belief
→ declaration through the Business Manifesto
→ final Business Foundation formalisation
→ brand expression
→ commercial communication
```

Specific convictions are the working handles used to reach the deeper belief. Do not reverse Langfell's hierarchy by treating belief as one item beneath a later conviction layer.

The manifesto tests whether the emerging Foundation is strong and coherent enough to formalise. Once its implications are reconciled, the Business Manifesto becomes a required section of the client `business-foundation.md`, not a separate business authority or an additional module. The exact Foundation approval covers the definition and declaration together.

The Module 01 Builder Manifesto is a conviction-excavation source. It may contribute founder language and specific convictions, but it is not the whole-business manifesto. The Business Manifesto must also account for the buyer, market, offer, outcome, proof, distinction, route and economic decisions established by the wider source set.

## Why the manifesto exists

Strategic work can describe a business accurately without revealing whether it genuinely stands for anything. The manifesto tests whether the earlier work has produced conviction strong enough to govern action.

If the work says `We believe this`, ask what believing it requires the business to do. If it says `This matters`, ask what the business will protect because it matters. If it identifies a problem, ask what the business is no longer prepared to accept. If it identifies an alternative, ask whether leadership will stand behind it when doing so becomes inconvenient.

The manifesto forces the work across the threshold from understanding to commitment.

## The manifesto as a strategic test

Before drafting, test each proposed governing conviction:

- Is it genuinely held?
- What evidence supports it?
- Does it affect decisions?
- Does it create a boundary?
- Does it identify what the business protects and refuses?
- Does it help when two reasonable options compete?
- Would leadership defend it when inconvenient?
- Does acting on it have consequences?

If not, it may be a sentiment rather than a strategically useful conviction. Do not solve weak conviction with stronger copy. Flag the weakness and return it to the owning source.

## Governing principle

The manifesto must be:

> **Derived, not invented.**

Do not manufacture beliefs, causes, enemies, promises or provocative statements merely to make the writing feel bold.

Every declaration must trace to assessed evidence or a named upstream authority and remain compatible with the reconciled Foundation. Keep claims at their authorised evidence strength. Preserve qualifications where removing one would change the truth.

Do not dilute a strong conviction merely because safer language is available.

The objective is:

> **Maximum conviction within the boundary of truth.**

## What a Business Manifesto is

The Business Manifesto is the declaration layer of the Business Foundation.

The strategic foundation asks:

> **What is true and what has been decided?**

The manifesto asks:

> **Given what we now know to be true, what are we prepared to stand for?**

It makes choices visible, establishes boundaries and names ideas the business is prepared to defend. It tells the reader not only what the business believes, but what follows from believing it.

A strong manifesto creates the sense that a decision has been made, a line has been drawn and a direction has been chosen.

## What it is not

Do not write:

- a corporate About Us statement;
- a purpose statement expanded into paragraphs;
- a list of company values;
- generic brand positioning;
- motivational prose;
- an advertising concept;
- a Brand Story;
- a sales pitch;
- an explanation of every part of the strategy;
- inflated revolutionary language; or
- a collection of slogans.

The manifesto may later yield strong campaign expressions, but it must contain enough substance for those expressions to be earned.

## Find the governing convictions

Read the assessed evidence and emerging Foundation sources before writing. Identify the smallest number of convictions necessary to explain what this business stands for. Do not treat every belief or preference as equally important. Look for the specific convictions that reveal and apply the governing belief.

### What the business sees

What truth about its market, customers, category or way of operating does it recognise particularly clearly? What has become normal that should not be normal?

### What the business believes

What identity-level belief sits beneath its joined decisions? Which specific convictions helped reveal it?

### What the business rejects

Which behaviour, assumption, compromise, convention or accepted operating pattern is the business unwilling to accept?

### What the business protects

What must remain true even as circumstances change?

### What the business chooses

When two reasonable options compete, which belief determines the choice?

### What the business intends to change

What meaningful difference is it trying to create?

### What the business commits itself to

What will the business actually do because of these beliefs?

### What becomes possible

If the business acts according to these commitments, what changes for the customer, organisation or market at the strength current evidence supports?

Use three to seven governing convictions. For each, record the exact evidence and the strategic consequence of acting from it. Unsupported convictions remain uncertain; do not write them as established fact.

## Find the line in the sand

Find the strongest legitimate yes/no within the assessed source work:

> We believe this. We do not believe that.  
> We accept this. We refuse that.  
> This should continue. This should end.  
> This is what matters. This is what we will not compromise.

Do not create artificial confrontation. Where a real strategic opposition exists, make it visible. The reader should be able to identify where the business stands.

## Write with conviction

Use declarative language. Prefer:

- `We believe…`
- `We refuse…`
- `We will…`
- `This should not be normal.`
- `There is another way.`
- `This is where we stand.`

Avoid `We aim to`, `We seek to`, `We aspire to`, `We are passionate about` and `We hope to` unless uncertainty is genuinely part of the authorised meaning.

Do not hedge a belief merely to make it sound more corporate.

## Make the business speak

The manifesto should sound believable if the founder or leadership team read it aloud. Write with the rhythm of a person making a declaration, not a strategist writing a report.

Use short declarative sentences, occasional longer sentences for substance, deliberate repetition, controlled pauses, contrast, binary constructions and changes of pace. Isolate a sentence only when its emphasis is earned.

Do not turn every sentence into a one-line fragment. The manifesto needs rhythm, not gimmickry.

## Be bold and forthright

Push important thoughts to their strongest defensible form. A strong line may make a reader ask, `Can they really say that?`, followed by, `Yes. They can explain why.`

Boldness comes from clarity, specificity, commitment, refusal, consequence and choosing one position over another. It does not come from adjectives or hype.

Do not hide the point beneath clever writing. If the business believes, rejects or intends something, say it plainly.

## Make the stakes clear

Do not leave the manifesto at the level of philosophy. Show what happens if the old way continues, who carries the burden, what breaks, what becomes harder and what opportunity is lost.

Then show what becomes possible when the business acts according to its belief. Keep projected effects distinct from demonstrated results.

## Create movement

Build approximately through this progression without mechanically using these as headings:

1. **Recognition** — begin with something true the intended reader recognises.
2. **Tension** — expose the problem beneath that reality.
3. **Consequence** — show what happens when the problem continues.
4. **Refusal** — state what the business will no longer accept.
5. **Belief** — declare the alternative conviction.
6. **Direction** — show what follows from acting on that belief.
7. **Commitment** — make clear what the business will do.
8. **Declaration** — reach the strongest expression of the position.
9. **Beginning** — end with what becomes possible next.

The manifesto should feel as though it has gone somewhere.

## Use contrast only when earned

Where the sources support it, contrast can sharpen the position:

> Not sameness. Alignment.  
> Not rigidity. Clarity.  
> Not more activity. Better decisions.

Do not manufacture opposites for stylistic effect.

## Keep it human and specific

Use concrete, accessible language from real business conversations. Avoid unnecessary branding, consulting, organisational-theory, transformation and marketing terminology unless it is genuinely part of this business's language.

The manifesto should feel bold, certain, alive, direct, grounded, intelligent, human and forthright. It may be provocative without becoming theatrical, challenging without manufacturing rebellion and emotional without becoming sentimental. Give it confidence without swagger.

Avoid generic manifesto constructions such as:

- `We believe in a better tomorrow`;
- `We dare to dream`;
- `We are on a mission`;
- `Together we can`;
- `We challenge the status quo`;
- `We are changing the game`;
- `We exist to disrupt`;
- `The future starts here`;
- `We are passionate about`; and
- `We put people at the heart of everything`.

Use one only when the sources make a specific version unavoidable and meaningful.

## Tests

### Specificity test

Could a competitor publish this tomorrow without changing anything? If yes, return to the source work for distinctive beliefs, real tensions, proprietary language, strategic choices, specific refusals, unusual observations and consequences the business understands particularly well.

### Courage test

For every significant declaration ask:

1. Is this the strongest truthful version of the idea?
2. Would the authorised leadership genuinely stand behind it if challenged?

If the first answer is no, sharpen it. If the second answer is no, the line is false or the emerging Foundation contains an unresolved decision. Do not solve weak strategy with stronger copy. Return the weakness to its owning source.

### Spoken test

Read the manifesto as though the founder or leadership team were delivering it to employees, customers, partners, investors and the wider market. Ask whether it sounds spoken, builds naturally, earns its firmer moments, uses short lines purposefully and carries enough full sentences to make the argument.

Smooth awkward transitions, but do not polish away the edges. A manifesto should have pulse.

### Claims and trace test

Map every material declaration to assessed evidence or a named upstream source. Reject any unsupported cause, enemy, promise, result, superlative or future certainty. A declaration may be morally or strategically definite while an empirical claim remains qualified.

### Coherence test

Do not use the manifesto to smooth contradictions away. Ask:

- Does one declared belief conflict with another?
- Does the commercial model undermine something the business claims to protect?
- Does the manifesto promise something the operating model cannot support?
- Does the declaration contradict the evidence?
- Does one part imply a materially different business from another?

If yes, name the conflict and return it. The manifesto is a coherence test for the emerging Business Foundation.

## Relationship to the Business Foundation and later outputs

Use this hierarchy:

```text
Evidence, conviction and belief work
  establishes what can be supported and defended
  └── Business Manifesto
      declares the governing stand and exposes contradictions

Business Foundation
  formalises the reconciled definition, decisions and manifesto

Brand Strategy
  determines how that conviction is represented, experienced and recognised
  └── Brand Story
      turns the approved truth and brand direction into a coherent narrative

Commercial positioning and messaging
  make the value relevant and understandable to specific audiences

Campaigns and assets
  extract memorable expressions inside the approved worldview and their own briefs
```

Once the manifesto has been agreed, use its Foundation Implications to test and formalise the final Business Foundation. Ensure its declarations are reflected where applicable in the business definition, purpose, governing belief, principles, audience, offer, decision rules, operating model and boundaries. If the manifesto and emerging Foundation disagree, investigate the contradiction. Do not silently rewrite one to match the other.

Later communication does not need to copy the manifesto literally. It must not contradict its worldview or use its conviction to bypass offer, audience, proof, claims, channel, production or release discipline.

## One governing manifesto

Do not automatically create a Brand Manifesto.

Module 05 should reference the approved Business Manifesto. It may create a **Brand Declaration View** only when the Entry Brief identifies a distinct public declaration job that the Manifesto, Brand Strategy, Brand Story and Presentation View cannot perform. That view remains non-authoritative and may sharpen expression without changing the belief, stand, refusal, relationship or claims boundary.

Any such expression is non-authoritative. It must trace to the Business Manifesto, add no competing belief system and receive an explicit expression decision and lock. The business declares the belief. The brand gives that belief form.

## Relationship to Brand Story

Do not replace Brand Story with the Business Manifesto.

Brand Story explains:

> How did we get here? What problem are we responding to? Why does this business exist? What journey are we inviting people into?

The Business Manifesto declares:

> What do we believe? What do we reject? Where do we stand? What are we committed to doing?

Story creates understanding. Manifesto creates conviction. Where the Brand Story merely repeats the manifesto in softer language, consolidate the repetition while retaining the narrative job.

## Required output

### 1. Conviction Audit

Identify three to seven governing convictions supported by the evidence and strategic work. For each state:

- **Conviction:** [statement]
- **Evidence:** [what supports it]
- **Strategic consequence:** [what believing it requires the business to do]

Flag uncertainty. Do not proceed as though an unsupported conviction is established fact.

### 2. Manifesto Spine

State these diagnostic sentences before the manifesto:

- **What we see:** [one sentence]
- **What we reject:** [one sentence]
- **What we believe:** [one sentence]
- **What we protect:** [one sentence]
- **What we choose:** [one sentence]
- **What we intend to change:** [one sentence]
- **What we commit to:** [one sentence]
- **What becomes possible:** [one sentence]

These are diagnostic statements, not slogans or a separate authority.

### 3. Business Manifesto

Write the complete manifesto. There is no mandatory word count. Use approximately 400–800 words when the Foundation contains enough substance. Use fewer words when the argument becomes stronger. Do not pad. Leave enough substance for the manifesto to establish stakes and earn its declaration.

### 4. Governing Declarations

Extract the three to five strongest declarations from the finished manifesto. These statements may govern later positioning, commercial language and campaign development. Do not invent new slogans. Extract only what the manifesto itself has earned.

### 5. Foundation Implications

Identify the implications the manifesto creates for the final Business Foundation:

- what must appear in the Foundation;
- which decisions need strengthening;
- which contradictions need resolving;
- which claims require evidence;
- which operating principles logically follow; and
- what the business can no longer credibly say or do.

Each implication must be reflected in the final Foundation, returned to its owning source or recorded as unresolved and blocking.

### 6. Source Trace

For each Conviction Audit item, Manifesto Spine statement and Governing Declaration, record the exact evidence or upstream source that authorises it. Keep this trace transient or in the compact run control. Do not expose technical source machinery in the clean client reading path.

## Final validation

Before presenting the manifesto, test truth, conviction, courage, specificity, consequence, coherence, humanity, rhythm, commercial relevance and expression. Confirm that every major declaration traces to evidence or source authority; the business takes a real position; strong ideas have not been unnecessarily weakened; acting on the beliefs changes behaviour; the emerging Foundation can support the declaration; and later brand, positioning, messaging, campaign and sales work can derive from it without inventing a different worldview.

## Final standard

The completed manifesto should leave the reader with the sense that the business is saying:

> We have looked at the evidence.  
> We know what we believe.  
> We know what that belief requires of us.  
> We know what we will not accept.  
> We know where we stand.  
> And we are prepared to build from it.

If it merely sounds impressive, rewrite it. If it sounds like marketing, go deeper. If it could belong to anybody, make it more specific. If it explains without declaring, make it bolder. If it declares without support, return to the Foundation.

The result should not feel as though the business has written a manifesto. It should feel as though the business has made a decision.
