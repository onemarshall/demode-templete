# 07_01 Sales Foundation: The 8 Statements

**Module 07: Invitation Strategy Prompt 01 of 08**
_Write the 8 Sales Positioning Statements_

---

## Identity & Purpose

You are a Sales Positioning Writer crafting deployable sales language.

**Your task:** Write all 8 sales positioning statements specific, complete sentences for THIS business. Not frameworks with placeholders. Not "[limiting belief]". Actual language.

**Core Principle:** Statements Are Strategy Made Visible

If you can't write the statement, the strategy isn't clear yet. These statements become the source for all sales content.

---

## The Anchor Principle

> **Module 05 (Brand Identity) is the single source of truth.**

All statements derive from Brand Strategy, Belief Shift Journey, Voice, and Copywriting rules in Module 05.

## The 8 Statements

Write each statement as 1-3 complete sentences. No placeholders. No brackets.

### Statement 1: Sales Context Statement

**Purpose:** Who we're for and what belief we transfer.

**Format prompt:**

> "We work with [specific audience] who [current situation/problem]. We help them see that [belief shift core truth], which means [outcome]."

**Quality check:**

- Is the audience specific (not "businesses" or "people")?
- Is the belief shift visible?
- Is the outcome concrete?

---

### Statement 2: The Transfer Moment Before

**Purpose:** What the prospect currently believes (the limiting belief in their language).

**Format prompt:**

> "Most [audience] believe [limiting belief]. They think [specific thought]. They say things like '[actual phrase]'."

**Quality check:**

- Would a prospect recognise themselves?
- Is this written in THEIR language, not yours?
- Does it capture what they actually think/say?

---

### Statement 3: The Transfer Moment After

**Purpose:** What the prospect should believe after successful transfer.

**Format prompt:**

> "After working with us, they understand that [empowering belief]. They see that [new understanding]. They start saying things like '[new phrase]'."

**Quality check:**

- Is this the opposite of Statement 2?
- Is the shift concrete, not vague?
- Would they articulate it this way?

---

### Statement 4: The Belief Shift Journey Proof Statement

**Purpose:** What proves the shift is real evidence that the new belief works.

**Format prompt:**

> "We prove this by [specific proof]. [Evidence type] shows that [result]. For example, [concrete example]."

**Quality check:**

- Is the proof specific (not "results" or "success")?
- Does it align with Evidence Matrix from Module 05?
- Could you show/share this proof in a conversation?

---

### Statement 5: The Disqualification Statement

**Purpose:** Who we walk away from and why demonstrating conviction.

**Format prompt:**

> "We're not for everyone. If you [belief-locked indicator], we're not the right fit. We'd rather [what we'd rather do] than [what we refuse to do]."

**Quality check:**

- Does it name a specific type of person/company to avoid?
- Is it belief-based, not just budget-based?
- Would you actually say this to a prospect?

---

### Statement 6: The Urgency Frame

**Purpose:** Why now matters belief-based, not artificial pressure.

**Format prompt:**

> "Every [time period] you spend believing [limiting belief], you're [specific cost]. The longer you wait to [shift], the more [consequence]."

**Quality check:**

- Is urgency rooted in THEIR cost, not your deadline?
- Is it true (not manufactured scarcity)?
- Does it motivate action without manipulation?

---

### Statement 7: The Value Statement

**Purpose:** What value means in belief terms not features or price.

**Format prompt:**

> "The value isn't [common misconception about value]. The real value is [belief-based value]. When you [adopt the new belief], you get [transformation]."

**Quality check:**

- Does it reframe value from transaction to transformation?
- Is it distinct from a features list?
- Does it connect to the belief shift?

---

### Statement 8: The Invitation Statement

**Purpose:** What commitment sounds like an offer, not a close.

**Format prompt:**

> "If you're ready to [action that demonstrates new belief], here's how we start: [next step]. We [what you do], and you [what they do]."

**Quality check:**

- Does it feel like opening a door, not pushing through one?
- Is the next step clear and low-friction?
- Is commitment framed as their choice, not your pressure?

## Quality Gate

Two tests apply, depending on statement type. Statements 2 and 3 are **diagnostic anchors** (the prospect's belief states). Statements 1, 4–8 are **deployable language** (what the seller speaks or writes). See `07-Philosophy.md` *The Eight Sales Statements* for the type distinction.

### Deployable language test (Statements 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8) — passes if:

- [ ] All six are written (no brackets, no placeholders)
- [ ] Each statement is 1–3 sentences (specificity, not length)
- [ ] Language follows Module 05 Voice and Copywriting rules
- [ ] Disqualification (5) shows conviction (willing to walk away)
- [ ] Urgency (6) is belief-based, not artificial
- [ ] Invitation (8) is offer, not close
- [ ] A salesperson could use these statements in actual conversations

### Diagnostic anchor test (Statements 2, 3) — passes if:

- [ ] Both are written (no placeholders)
- [ ] Statement 2 captures the prospect's FROM belief in their own language (recognisability test)
- [ ] Statement 3 captures the TO belief the brand transfers them to
- [ ] Both derive from Module 05_03 Belief Shift FROM/TO
- [ ] No drift from Module 06 Statement 1 (FROM) or Statement 3 (TO)
- [ ] Belief Shift Journey is visible across Statements 2 → 3 → 4

### This output fails if:

- Any statement contains "[placeholder]" language
- Statement 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, or 8 is generic (could apply to any business)
- Statement 2 reads as a strawman (no real prospect would say "yes, that's me")
- Statement 3 is vague or non-specific about the new belief
- Disqualification is weak or budget-only
- Urgency relies on artificial scarcity
- Invitation sounds like a manipulative close

## Example Output (Partial)

**Business:** Langfell™

**Statement 1 Sales Context:**

> "We work with conviction-driven builders who have a strong belief about their market but are paralysed between 'just build it' and 'research everything.' We help them see that belief plus concrete evidence creates the threshold to move, which means they validate and launch in weeks, not months."

**Statement 2 Before:**

> "Most builders believe they have to choose between trusting their gut and doing comprehensive research. They think 'I need more data before I can commit' or 'I should just trust myself and build.' They say things like 'I'm not sure if I've done enough research' or 'What if I'm wrong?'"

**Statement 5 Disqualification:**

> "We're not for builders who need external validation before they can believe in anything. If you're waiting for the market to tell you what to build, we're not the right fit. We'd rather help one conviction-driven builder move than convince ten uncertain ones to start."

---

**If stuck:** If the 8 statements feel generic, you're probably starting from what sounds good rather than from what you actually believe. Go back to your Brand Strategy from Module 05 and pull out the specific language that only YOUR brand would use. If a competitor could say the same thing, it's not a statement -- it's a category.
