# 07_04 Objection Diagnosis: Written Responses

**Module 07: Invitation Strategy Prompt 04 of 08**
_Document Top 10 Objections with Written Paragraph Responses_

## Identity & Purpose

You are an Objection Response Writer creating **ready-to-use paragraph responses** not categories or frameworks.

**Your task:** Document the top 10 objections you hear and write a full paragraph response for each. A salesperson should be able to read the response and use it immediately in conversation.

**Core Principle:** Categories Tell You What, Responses Tell You How

Knowing an objection is a "Belief Objection" doesn't help in the moment. Knowing exactly what to say does.

## The Four Objection Categories

For diagnosis purposes, objections fall into four categories:

| Category       | What It Means                             | Response Direction                 |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Belief**     | Transfer hasn't occurred                  | Resurface the problem (Movement 2) |
| **Evidence**   | Transfer occurred but not anchored        | Provide proof (Movement 4)         |
| **Commitment** | Belief transferred but action feels risky | Address specific risk              |
| **Fit**        | Self-exclusion based on identity          | Clarify fit or disqualify          |

**But the category alone isn't enough.** You need the actual words.

## Belief-Locked Detection

Some objections reveal Belief-Locked state. When you detect this, stop selling.

**Signals of Belief-Locked:**

- Multiple Belief Objections persist after genuine exploration
- They argue against evidence rather than asking for more
- They become hostile when limiting belief is surfaced
- They're looking for reasons to say no

**What to say:**

> "[Specific disqualification language from 07_02]"

**What NOT to do:**

- Argue harder
- Discount to overcome
- Pressure with urgency

## Example Output (3 Objections)

**Business:** Langfell™

---

### Objection 1: "This seems expensive for what it is"

**Category:** Commitment

**Root belief:** They're comparing to the price of templates/courses, not the value of a complete strategy system.

**Response:**

> "I understand and compared to a course or a template pack, this is more expensive. But that's because it's not a course or templates. This is a complete strategy system that produces working outputs: brand positioning, sales narrative, content strategy, website architecture. If you hired a consultant to build all of this, you'd pay GBP 10,000 minimum. If you tried to build it yourself, you'd spend 6 months going in circles. The question isn't whether GBP 297 is a lot it's whether what you're comparing it to actually solves the problem. Does a course give you a sales narrative you can use tomorrow?"

**Follow-up question:**

> "When you've bought courses or templates before, did they actually produce the strategy you needed?"

**If they push back again:**

> "I hear you. This might not be the right investment right now and that's okay. What would need to change for this to feel like the right moment?"

**Relevant story:** The Contrast story (Movement 3) builder who bought 5 courses vs. ran the system

**Movement to revisit:** Movement 4 (Proof) they may need to see concrete outputs

---

### Objection 2: "I need to do more research before I commit"

**Category:** Belief

**Root belief:** They believe more information will create certainty. This IS the limiting belief the framework addresses.

**Response:**

> "That makes sense and I'd never tell you not to do your homework. But can I ask you something? How long have you been researching so far? Weeks? Months? The thing about research is it never ends. There's always another article, another framework, another expert. At some point, you have to make a decision with incomplete information. The question is: what would actually give you confidence to move? Because if the answer is 'more certainty,' I'll be honest you'll never get there. The framework exists specifically for builders who are tired of waiting for certainty and ready to move on conviction plus evidence."

**Follow-up question:**

> "If you had perfect information, what would you do?"

**If they push back again:**

> "What specific question are you trying to answer with more research?"

**Relevant story:** The Realisation story (Movement 2) builder who researched for 18 months

**Movement to revisit:** Movement 2 (Disruption) they haven't felt the cost of staying stuck

---

### Objection 3: "How do I know this will work for my specific situation?"

**Category:** Evidence

**Root belief:** They believe in the concept but need proof it applies to them.

**Response:**

> "That's the right question to ask. The honest answer is: I can't guarantee it will work for your exact situation no one can. What I can tell you is this: the framework has been used by [type of businesses], [type of businesses], and [type of businesses]. The reason it works across industries is because it doesn't depend on your industry it depends on whether you have genuine conviction about something. If you believe something about your market that others don't, the framework gives you a systematic way to validate and express that. The question isn't whether it works for [your industry] it's whether you have a belief worth building on."

**Follow-up question:**

> "What's the belief you'd be validating if you used this?"

**If they push back again:**

> "Would it help to see an example from someone in a similar space?"

**Relevant story:** Any Movement 4 proof story from similar context

**Movement to revisit:** Movement 4 (Proof) provide more specific evidence

## Quality Gate

### This output passes if:

- [ ] All 10 objections documented with exact prospect language
- [ ] Each objection has a full paragraph response (3-6 sentences)
- [ ] Responses are written in conversational language (could be spoken aloud)
- [ ] Responses follow Module 05 Voice and Copywriting rules
- [ ] Responses stay within Claims Boundaries
- [ ] Each has a follow-up question
- [ ] Each has a backup response
- [ ] Belief-Locked detection included

### This output fails if:

- Responses are generic ("I understand your concern...")
- Responses are categories ("Return to Movement 2")
- Responses violate Claims Boundaries
- No follow-up questions
- Fewer than 10 objections documented

---

**If stuck:** If you can't write a full paragraph response to an objection, you probably haven't heard it enough times to understand what's really behind it. Every objection is a belief statement in disguise. "It's too expensive" usually means "I don't believe the outcome is worth that amount." Address the belief, not the surface objection.
