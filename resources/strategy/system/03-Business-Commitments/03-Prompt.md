# Module 03: Business Commitments Prompt

Its Input, Output, collection, asset and private run destinations override legacy flat output examples. Keep existing authority and approval gates unchanged.


## Client-language translation gate

Apply the `FW_27` Client-language translation gate to every client-facing strategy document. Preserve the strategic choices, alternatives and evidence limits, but explain them as decisions the founder can use rather than as framework categories.

## Meaning Record requirement

Apply `FW_35` before locking a material strategy interpretation or choice. The founder confirms the practical choice, trade-off and consequence; market evidence remains evidence-bounded. Do not let the strategy label replace the situation it is meant to change.

## FW_32 accountability requirements — required

Apply `framework/FW_32-Strategy-Accountability-Implementation-and-Decision-Support-Contract.md`.

For every material accepted strategic choice, create one exact decision record and, where the FW_32 materiality rule is met:

- one Implementation Consequence for each distinct required, optional, conditional, deferred or prohibited work implication;
- one non-monetary Cost Driver for each estimable work unit; and
- one compact Decision Measurement Contract stating the problem, diagnosis evidence and contradiction, intended observable effect, implementation proof, smallest useful baseline requirement, candidate leading/progression/lagging evidence, attribution ceiling, evidence owner, earliest review, strong/partial/weak decisions, stop condition and narrowest strategic return point.

Do not promise the expected effect, set current-cycle quantities or monetary rates, or combine strategic, production, publication, budget and spend approval.

### Strategic judgement block — required

After the commitment, proof, friction and advocate analysis—and before the final approval—render one client-facing Strategic Judgement section using FW_32's judgement sequence:

1. **Options considered** — present the credible alternatives separately, including the status quo or no-action route where legitimate. Do not invent weak alternatives.
2. **Recommended option** — make one direct professional call and explain why it is preferred now.
3. **Commitment confidence** — `high`, `moderate` or `low`, followed immediately by the evidence, counterevidence, unknowns, consequence and reversibility that earn that level. It is confidence in this decision at this scope, not probability of market success.
4. **Commercial consequences** — state what becomes possible if the commitment holds, what the business is exposed to if it does not, and what delay or no decision means. Do not invent revenue, savings, market response or outcomes.
5. **Reversal conditions** — name the exact evidence, event or operating condition that would weaken, reverse or reopen the recommendation.
6. **Integrity check** — state whether any conviction, buyer, evidence, claim or operating boundary was weakened merely to make the business easier to build or sell. Any such weakening returns to the owning source; it cannot be hidden as pragmatic positioning.
7. **Professional call** — `proceed`, `proceed conditionally`, `gather evidence`, `return` or `do not proceed`, with the exact next named work or return owner.

The client should be able to answer: what options were considered, why this route was selected, how strong the recommendation is, what is at stake, what would change the advice and what they are being asked to approve.

## What This Prompt Does

This prompt guides Module 03 facilitation. It helps turn conviction and market reconnaissance into a defensible Business Commitments strategy, with proof commitments and a friction assessment.

**How to use:** Provide this prompt to an AI assistant along with your Module 01 outputs (Core Belief, Conviction elements) and Module 02 outputs (Industry Default, vacuum hypotheses). The AI will help you design business position with belief rationale and proof commitments.

**Reality check:** AI cannot manufacture conviction where none exists. It can only structure what you've already excavated. If your Module 01 work was surface-level, this module will expose that gap.

**Input:** Module 01 Handoff Pack (Core Belief + all five conviction elements + draft Claims Boundary Inputs) + Module 02 Reconnaissance Log (Outcome A)
**Output:** Business Commitments with Conviction Grid, proof commitments, and draft Belief Shift Journey (Belief Shift Journey is finalised in Module 05)
**Time:** 90–120 minutes with AI assistance

### Step 1: Review User Inputs

- Review the user's Core Belief from Module 01 (should be in Handoff Pack).
- Review the five conviction elements: Broken Thing, Hidden Truth, Non-Negotiable, Hard-Won Teaching, Legacy.
- Review Module 02 outputs: Industry Default identification, vacuum hypotheses, customer pain evidence.
- **Review the Second-Voice Diagnostic Signal from Module 02 Step 1b.** If a second voice with operational visibility (co-founder, spouse-operator, contractor, senior delivery staff) offered a competing diagnosis to the founder's stated problem, ingest the split. The diagnostic-signal verdict tells you whose framing the positioning is solving for. If the second voice's framing was more diagnostic, the Belief Challenge (Step 2) and Conviction Grid (Step 4) should be designed against *that* problem-shape, not the founder's stated one.
- **Review the Cyclical-vs-Structural Diagnosis from Module 02 Terrain Pre-Check.** If the diagnosis is **Cyclical**, flag immediately — Module 03 may be premature; cyclical demand softness is rarely solved by repositioning, and a Conviction Grid built to explain a temporary down-cycle will be rebuilt on an artefact of timing. If **Structural-negative**, narrow the positioning timeline — share-capture window is constrained. If **Structural-positive**, proceed normally. If **Ambiguous**, surface the dependency in Step 8 (Cost of Commitment Check), because the cost of commitment lands very differently against the three terrain readings.
- **Review the Acknowledged Structural Risks from Module 02 Gate 5.** Each risk routed to Module 03 must land somewhere in this run — typically Friction Analysis (Step 6) and Risk Flags (Step 9). Each risk routed to Modules 04, 06, or 11 must be carried forward unchanged in the Handoff Pack, not silently dropped. The Acknowledgments signed by the builder in Module 02 are binding — Module 03 may not contradict or weaken them.
- **Review the Contrarian Trap flag from Module 02 Step 9 (Risk Flags).** If Module 02 flagged the vacuum claim as a Potential Contrarian Trap — rejecting the Default for being common, being different for difference's sake, vacuum not grounded in evidenced pain, or rebuttal relying on opinion — **halt Module 03 and route the user back to Module 01 / Module 02 to refine the Hidden Truth before any Conviction Grid is built.** Building a Conviction Grid on a category-conventional Hidden Truth produces a procedurally complete strategy that does not survive Step 8b (Advocate Challenges). Catching this at Step 1 saves the user 4–6 hours of grid construction on a suspect foundation.
- Verify that conviction and market opportunity align. If they don't, flag this immediately.

### Step 2: Define the Belief Challenge

- Articulate what the industry believes (the Industry Default from Module 02).
- Articulate what the user believes (their Core Belief from Module 01).
- Identify the specific points of contradiction between these beliefs.
- Verify: Is this a genuine belief difference, or contrarianism for its own sake? (See Contrarian Trap in Module 02.)

### Step 3: Design the Belief Shift Journey (FROM → TO)

- Format: FROM "[limiting belief customers currently hold]" TO "[empowering belief your brand offers]"
- Ensure the FROM belief is specific enough that customers recognise themselves.
- Ensure the TO belief is liberating (not just different) and produces experiential change.
- Apply the Tylenol test: What changes in the customer's actual experience when they shift FROM → TO? The test is shaped for *experiential change*, not just *opinion change* — but "experience" comes in different shapes depending on the business:
    - **Product / consumer / physical experience.** What does the customer notice in their body, their environment, the artefact in front of them? (Original Tylenol shape: faster pain relief, smaller pill, fewer side effects.)
    - **Community / service / relational experience.** What does the customer notice in their relationships, conversations, the room they're in, the people they have access to? (AdvisorLoop shape: who's in the discussion, what the discussion is about, whether they had to perform for prospects to be there, who they can ask for practice help without being sold to.)
    - **B2B / infrastructure / operational experience.** What does the customer notice in their workflow, their ability to change vendors, their data ownership, their operational independence over a multi-year horizon? (Voltway shape: changing the network without ripping out the install, owning the data, deciding without asking the vendor.)
    - **Knowledge / informational experience.** What does the customer now know they didn't before? What can they explain that they previously had to take on trust? (Founder-facing knowledge products.)
- For each business, pick the shape that fits. The test passes when the experiential change is *describable in the customer's own voice* in a single quoted paragraph, not in marketing-speak. If the only way to describe the change is at the level of belief or opinion, the Belief Shift Journey hasn't reached experiential level yet — push further.
- Define the **customer discovery mechanism**: what the customer can do, see, compare or answer to discover the gap themselves. Where a diagnostic is appropriate, state what it must reveal and which proof it connects to. Module 03 defines the strategic job; Module 07 designs the in-room mechanic.

### Step 4: Build the Conviction Grid

Create four strategic actions, each with belief rationale and proof commitment:

| Action        | Belief Rationale (Why)                                     | Proof Commitment (How)                              |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **QUESTION**  | What belief says this is necessary -- and why is it wrong? | How will we demonstrate our alternative?            |
| **REJECT**    | What belief says this must be high -- and why is it false? | How will we prove our lower/cleaner approach works? |
| **COMMIT**    | What conviction drives us to over-invest here?             | What will we demonstrate that competitors won't?    |
| **INTRODUCE** | What new truth are we introducing to the market?           | How will we prove this truth is real?               |

**Critical:** Each action must have BOTH belief rationale and proof commitment. Missing either means the action is incomplete.

**Operational specificity (required).** Once the four-row table is complete, turn each Proof Commitment into **one rule another team can apply without re-deciding it**. A policy such as *"refuses vendor sponsorship"* is too broad for Modules 04, 06, 08 and 09 to use directly. Without a shared operating rule, each module must invent its own interpretation, and those interpretations can drift apart.

Push each row to operational-rule level:

- *Policy-level (insufficient):* "Refuses vendor sponsorship."
- *Operational-rule level (sufficient):* "Refuses paid promotion of any software vendor in monthly resource pack content; will identify but not endorse vendors in member discussions; podcast and conference appearances permitted if no payment changes hands."

The test: hand the operational rule to someone not in the room. Can they apply it to a new case without asking a clarifying question? If yes, the rule is portable. If no, push further.

Record the operational rule alongside the proof commitment in the Grid output. Modules 04, 06, 08, and 09 will read it directly.

For every material QUESTION and REJECT move, also name a **Public Specialisation Signal**: a visible policy, named method, diagnostic, framework, public point of view, operating practice, specialist body of thinking or other proof artefact that embodies the refusal. Test: *Could the market observe what we question or refuse without reading this Grid?* If not, record the visibility gap for downstream resolution.

For relevant COMMIT and INTRODUCE moves, name the **deployable proof artefact type** through which the commitment could become visible. This is a strategic specification, not final content, sales or product execution.

### Step 5: Define Proof Points (Proof Score)

For each strategic action, identify specific proof points:

**Product Proof:** Features, limitations, or design choices that embody the belief
**Policy Proof:** Rules, terms, or procedures that prove you mean it
**Story Proof:** Specific incidents where you acted on the belief when it cost you
**Named Framework / Diagnostic Proof:** A concrete methodology, model, diagnostic, structured tool or operating framework through which the belief becomes observable, teachable, usable and potentially referenceable. A branded name without a substantive method is not proof.

Record the proof economy for each commitment:

- **Discrete Proof-at-Cost** — one decision that costs revenue, customers, capability, convenience or optionality.
- **Compounding Public Proof** — a sustained record of research, teaching, publishing, frameworks, diagnostics, cases, documented practice or repeated decisions. Individual acts may be inexpensive; cumulative time, consistency, vulnerability, opportunity cost and reputational commitment carry the cost.

**Use the score only as an inventory heuristic.** Three distinct proof commitments can expose a thin evidence base, but the count is not a universal readiness threshold. For each proposed public claim, record the evidence type, provenance, inspectability, limitations and the sentence it can earn. Marketing permission comes from the approved claim and matched evidence strength, rights and release gate — never from the count alone.

**Handling thin Story Proof.** Apply the decision-specific threshold in FW_03. Story Proof supports a claim of past behaviour; its absence is not a universal veto on selecting a new strategic commitment.

- **Pre-launch or newly introduced practice:** retain the row as prospective. Record the owner, portable operating rule, cost or trade-off, plausible delivery mechanism, strongest counterevidence, next qualifying incident to capture and review trigger. It may support a bounded strategic choice if the Module 03 validation tests pass and the missing enactment does not undermine that choice. It does not support a claim of established adherence or outcomes.
- **Existing behaviour not previously narrated:** inspect the actual incidents and their provenance. Name people, date, cost and the belief demonstrated. Attributed founder testimony remains attributed; reframing does not upgrade its evidence strength.
- **Entirely aspirational strategy:** test whether conviction constrains any actual or prospective decision and whether the proposed mechanism can plausibly serve the buyer. Keep Provisional status where missing evidence makes the next decision unsupported; return to Outcome X if the advocate challenge exposes preference without customer value or a fundamental upstream problem. Never invent enactment or lock a strategy merely because its prose is coherent.

Record evidence status row by row. Do not require one historic Story Proof per Grid action or let one untested new practice downgrade otherwise supported commitments automatically. The Quality Gates' three validation tests remain mandatory for Validated status. That status applies to the tested strategic choice and evidence scope, not to every promised operating behaviour. Modules 06–10 must inherit prospective practices and their claim restrictions unchanged.

### Step 6: Assess Friction

For each strategic action, assess what it would cost competitors to copy:

| Friction Source      | Assessment Question                                          |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Belief contradiction | Would they have to contradict their past claims?             |
| Operational change   | Would they need to restructure how they work?                |
| Cultural shift       | Would their team resist this change?                         |
| Customer explanation | Would they need to explain a reversal to existing customers? |
| Economic cost | What revenue, margin, capability or optionality must they sacrifice? |
| Accumulated proof | What body of enacted or public proof would they need to reproduce? |
| Specialist authority | What earned referenceability or cohort standing would they need to build? |

**High friction = multiple interconnected changes required**
**Low friction = single change (feature, price) and they match you**

**Lowest-friction check (required).** After completing the friction table, identify the competitor with the *lowest* total friction score. **That competitor is the realistic copy-threat.** The biggest competitor may face the highest friction because its business model depends most heavily on the Default. A smaller incumbent, adjacent-category competitor or well-funded new entrant may have much less to change.

If the lowest-friction competitor differs from the one the user has planned against, raise that immediately. Name this threat in Risk Flags (Step 9) and assess differentiation against it separately. Friction alone will not protect the position from that competitor.

Do not call any source a permanent moat. Public proof and specialist authority buy time and credibility only while the builder continues to maintain them.

### Step 6b: Market Credibility Condition

Keep **Strategic Lock** separate from **Market Credibility**. Record evidence of independent reference, specialty recognition, qualified inbound, framework reuse or citation, prospect problem recognition, and association of the position with the builder.

Where evidence is thin, record a `credibility-gap` and its likely cause: insufficient time or proof, weak distribution, poor execution, weak recognition, or a genuine strategic challenge. A credibility gap does not unlock the Grid. Only the existing re-decoding conditions can do that.

### Step 7: Honest Confrontation Check

#### Step 7a: Past-Behaviour Audit (before future-facing confrontations)

Before reviewing where the user must confront *going forward*, audit where they have *already* acted in ways that contradict the candidate Core Belief. Past behaviour is evidence about whether the belief is genuinely held or merely stated.

Ask the user to list, for the last 12–24 months:

- Press placements, podcast appearances, awards accepted
- Partnerships entered or sponsorships taken
- Customers won where the customer's stated motivation contradicts the belief
- Public marketing or web copy currently live

For each entry, ask: **does the candidate Core Belief say *yes* or *no* to this?**

**Any past acceptance the belief says *no* to is a contradiction.** Surface every contradiction explicitly. Each contradiction forces one of two decisions:

- **(a) Refine the belief** to match actual behaviour. The current candidate Core Belief is more aspirational than honest; restate it to describe what the business actually does and believes. Route this finding back into Module 01 if it changes the underlying conviction. *This may also trigger Outcome X if the refined belief loses its load-bearing differentiation.*
- **(b) Commit publicly to a different posture going forward.** The belief is genuinely held; the past behaviour is what the business is moving *away* from. Document the contradiction and the explicit pivot — the contradiction itself becomes a Story Proof candidate (the moment the business stopped accepting the contradiction is the proof).

**Do not leave contradictions un-decided.** A candidate Core Belief that contradicts more than one past behaviour without a documented pivot is a belief the business doesn't actually hold yet. This complements Step 5's *positioning-not-yet-public* Story Proof handling — same audit period, different lens.

#### Step 7b: Future-facing Confrontations

Review the strategy for places where honest confrontation is required going forward:

- Where must the user say "no" to customers who want what the industry offers?
- Where must they reject partnerships or opportunities that violate their belief?
- Where have they stayed silent when they should speak?

Reference the Teenage Engineering/Volvo example from `03-Philosophy.md`.

### Step 8: Cost of Commitment Check

Review the strategy for genuine costs:

- What revenue is being left on the table?
- What opportunities are being rejected?
- What would competitors happily do that you're refusing to do?

Reference the Basecamp example from `03-Philosophy.md`. If there are no costs, the commitment isn't real.

### Step 8b: Advocate Challenges

Before finalising the strategy, subject the positioning to these direct challenges:

1. **"What if the Industry Default is right?"** -- Articulate the strongest case for why the current market belief works. If you can't, you don't understand what you're challenging.
2. **"What happens when a well-funded competitor copies this positioning?"** -- If your friction analysis says "they can't," push harder. What if they can -- and do?
3. **"Is this positioning or just preference?"** -- Positioning creates value for customers. Preference is just what you like. Which is this?

**Honest limitation:** Strategic positioning is a bet. This framework helps you make it deliberately, with evidence and conviction. It cannot guarantee a market response.

### Step 9: Output the Business Commitments

Before structuring the output, decide which outcome the strategy lands at. There are two valid outcomes from Module 03.

**Outcome A — Business Commitments Locked.** The Conviction Grid is complete; every proposed claim has evidence proportionate to its consequence or is explicitly marked provisional with a capture and review plan; Friction Analysis passes the lowest-friction check; and Step 8b (Advocate Challenges) does not reveal a fundamental conviction or positioning problem. A Proof Score may be recorded as an inventory aid but cannot create this outcome. Proceed to Module 04 (Viability Check).

**Outcome X — Strategy Cannot Be Locked (Upstream Refinement Required).** Fires when one or more of the following surfaces during the run:

- Step 1 detected a Contrarian Trap flag inherited from Module 02 Step 9 → route back to Module 01 to refine the Hidden Truth (and re-run Module 02 against the refined Truth).
- Step 8b revealed that the Industry Default's strongest case is more defensible than the user's rebuttal — the Hidden Truth doesn't survive its own challenge → route back to Module 01.
- Step 8b revealed the positioning is preference, not positioning — no customer value claim, only founder taste → route back to Module 01.
- Step 8b revealed the friction analysis was built on a false moat (Step 6's lowest-friction check failed, and the positioning has no real differentiation against the lowest-friction competitor) → route back to Module 02 to refine the vacuum candidate, or to Module 01 to refine the Hidden Truth.

**Outcome X is not failure.** It is the framework working as designed — Module 03's checks catching upstream thinness before downstream work locks against it. The user has saved 4–6 hours of grid construction on a suspect foundation, plus the much larger downstream cost of Modules 04–10 building on top.

**If Outcome X fires, do not produce a Business Commitments strategy.** Produce a structured return document naming:

1. The upstream module to return to (01 or 02)
2. What needs refining: for example, Hidden Truth, Core Belief or the vacuum candidate
3. The evidence from this run that the upstream work needs
4. An explicit instruction not to lock any Module 03 output for downstream use

Record the result on separate axes:

- `provisional` authorises Module 04 hypothesis-mode analysis only; it cannot authorise Module 05 lock or a fully validated Business Foundation strategy field;
- `validated` authorises only its named current scope and still requires Module 04 and the independent Business Foundation gate;
- `outcome_x` records a valid return decision but cannot create an authorised strategy handoff;
- `version_locked` never upgrades provisional evidence.

- Gate 2: Proof Match Test -- Does each proposed claim have evidence of the right type, strength, provenance and inspectability for its intended use?
- Gate 3: Friction Assessment -- Is copy friction identified?
- Gate 4: Belief Shift Clarity -- Is FROM → TO specific and experiential?
- Gate 5: Conviction Grid Completeness -- Do all actions have belief + proof?
- Gate 6: Validation Standard -- Can you make the complete statement?

**Tone Reminder:** Be direct about gaps ("This proof point is vague -- you need specifics"), grounded in the user's actual conviction ("Your Core Belief supports this action because..."), and warm without false reassurance ("This is hard work, and you're doing it. Let's get it right.").

**Final Note:** If the user's Module 01 or Module 02 outputs are weak or incomplete, state this clearly. Do not build positioning on a weak foundation. Guide them back to the earlier modules rather than proceeding with surface-level strategy.

## Warning: AI Cannot Manufacture Conviction

AI can help structure conviction into defensible positioning. It cannot supply the conviction. If Module 01 remains superficial, polished strategy language will not repair it.

**Protect against this:**

- Verify every strategic action traces back to your Core Belief
- Check that proof commitments are things you've actually done or are definitively doing
- Ensure friction analysis is honest about what competitors would need to change
- Confirm the Belief Shift Journey produces real experiential change (Tylenol test)

A strong Business Commitments strategy combines AI-assisted structure with human judgement about what is believable and defensible.
