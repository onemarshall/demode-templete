# Module 02: Glossary

**DeMode™**
_Terminology Specific to Market Reality Check_

For master framework terms (Belief, Proof, Result, Vital 3), see `FW_07-Glossary.md`.

---

## Core Concepts

### Artifact

Observable evidence that reveals an invisible belief.

**Definition:** Elements you can point to -- pricing, policies, messaging, features -- that indicate what a company or market believes.

**Example:** A pricing page with 5 tiers based on feature count is an artifact of the belief "more features = more value."

**Usage Rule:** We decode beliefs from artifacts. No artifact = no claim.

---

### Belief Decoding

The process of inferring underlying beliefs from observable artifacts.

**Method:**

1. Identify the artifact (what can you see?)
2. Ask "what belief would produce this?"
3. Verify with multiple artifacts where possible
4. Assign confidence level

**Caution:** Always cite the specific artifact. Decoding without evidence is guessing.

---

### Industry Default Belief (Descriptive Default)

The unexamined assumption that governs how an entire industry operates.

**Definition:** The belief most competitors in the market share without questioning.

**Type of claim:** Empirical. Identifying it is research work; artifacts confirm it.

**Characteristics:**

- Widely accepted across competitors
- Rarely stated explicitly
- Shapes what's considered "normal" or "necessary"

**Examples:**

- "Customers want more features" (SaaS)
- "Lower price wins" (commodity markets)
- "Speed is the differentiator" (service industries)

**Critical Note:** A Descriptive Default may be correct. Research must establish separately whether it is _Vulnerable_ (see next term). Identifying a shared belief does not invalidate it.

---

### Vulnerable Default

Descriptive Defaults that there is reason to challenge.

**Definition:** A Descriptive Default whose failure is evidenced -- it creates customer pain that customers care about, and a different belief could credibly serve them better.

**Type of claim:** Normative. The judgement requires three kinds of evidence:

1. Evidence of the Descriptive Default itself
2. Customer pain traceable to that default
3. A plausible alternative

**Why the distinction matters:** Assuming a Descriptive Default is Vulnerable creates the Contrarian Trap: "Everyone believes X, so X is wrong." Some defaults are both common and correct. Those are not Vulnerable, and no vacuum exists against them.

**Test:** Could a competitor with genuine good faith defend the Descriptive Default? If they could, and you can't rebut them with evidence, the default is not (yet) Vulnerable.

---

### Limiting Belief (Customer)

A false assumption held by customers that prevents them from achieving their desired outcome.

**Definition:** What customers believe that holds them back.

**Example:** "Switching costs are always higher than staying."

**Why it matters:** Identifying limiting beliefs shows where changing a customer’s belief could create value.

---

### Vacuum

A specific gap where the market operates on a belief that creates pain -- and where a different belief could create value.

**Definition:** A belief gap with evidence of:

1. Pain (customers suffer from the current default)
2. Payment willingness (they would pay for relief)
3. Delivery capability (you can credibly provide the alternative)
4. Conviction alignment (it matches what you believe)

**Contrast with "gap":** A gap is any difference. A vacuum is a validated opportunity with belief foundation. Not every gap is a vacuum.

---

## Research Elements

### Terrain Analysis

Assessment of market health before diving into belief analysis.

**Components:** TAM (Total Addressable Market), growth trajectory, key trends, confidence level.

**Purpose:** Verify the market is worth entering before investing in deeper analysis.

---

### Competitor Belief Map

Structured analysis of what competitors believe, decoded from artifacts.

| Column             | Definition                   |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| **Competitor**     | Company name                 |
| **Artifact**       | Specific observable evidence |
| **Decoded Belief** | The inferred belief          |
| **Confidence**     | High / Medium / Low          |
| **Vulnerability**  | Why this belief might fail   |

---

### Customer Pain Profile

Psychographic analysis focused on beliefs and pain rather than demographics.

| Element                 | Definition                                        |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **The Trigger**         | The moment that creates the search for a solution |
| **The Hack**            | What they're using instead (often a workaround)   |
| **The Pain**            | What they hate about current options              |
| **The Limiting Belief** | The false assumption holding them back            |

---

### Belief Gap Analysis

The synthesis output identifying where conviction-based positioning might work.

**Components:**

- Industry Default Belief (what everyone assumes)
- Customer Frustration (what pain this creates)
- Potential Vacuum (where truth is ignored)
- Conviction Alignment (does it match your Core Belief?)

---

## Artifact Types

| Type                 | What to Look For                  | What It Reveals                       |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Pricing Artifact** | Structure, tiers, terms           | What they believe "value" means       |
| **Hero Artifact**    | Headlines, taglines, hero images  | What they believe customers fear/want |
| **Policy Artifact**  | Contracts, refunds, support terms | What they believe about relationships |
| **Feature Artifact** | What's emphasised, what's hidden  | What they believe matters most        |

---

## Confidence Levels

| Level      | Definition                                 | Evidence Standard                    |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| **High**   | Multiple artifacts confirm the same belief | 3+ consistent artifacts              |
| **Medium** | One clear artifact, behaviour supports it  | 1–2 artifacts + consistent behaviour |
| **Low**    | Inference based on limited evidence        | Single artifact or indirect signals  |

---

## Quality Terms

### Vulnerability

The weakness in a competitor's belief that creates opportunity.

**Definition:** Why a competitor's belief might fail customers or be successfully challenged.

**Example:** "Their belief that 'more features = value' is vulnerable because customers are overwhelmed and seeking simplicity."

---

### Gap Validation

The process of testing whether a belief gap represents a real opportunity.

**Three Questions:**

1. Do enough people feel this pain? (Evidence required)
2. Are they willing to pay for relief? (Evidence required)
3. Can we credibly deliver the alternative? (Capability required)

---

### Belief Gap Statement

The synthesis statement summarising reconnaissance findings.

**Format:**

> "The industry believes [X], evidenced by [artifacts]. This causes customers to experience [pain]. We see a potential vacuum for a brand that believes [Y]. Validation status: [Validated / Hypothesis]."

---

## Caution Terms

### Contrarian Trap

The mistake of assuming industry beliefs are wrong simply because they're common.

**Symptom:** "Everyone believes X, so X must be false."

**Reality:** Sometimes the consensus exists for good reason.

**Fix:** Test whether your alternative is actually better, not just different.

---

### Hypothesis vs. Validated

| Term           | Definition                                                  | Language to Use                          |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Hypothesis** | Belief decoded but not yet confirmed with customer evidence | "We believe..." / "Evidence suggests..." |
| **Validated**  | Confirmed through direct customer evidence                  | "Customers confirm..." / "Data shows..." |

---

## Output Terms

### Reconnaissance Log

The structured output document containing all findings.

**Sections:**

1. Terrain (market health)
2. Landscape (competitor beliefs)
3. Target (customer pain profile)
4. Belief Gap Assessment
5. Leverage Assessment
6. Synthesis (Belief Gap Statement)
7. Belief-First Validation Check

## Related Master Terms

These terms are defined in `FW_07-Glossary.md`:

- **Belief** (builder input) -- The conviction that authorises all strategy
- **Proof** (builder input) -- The behaviour that demonstrates the belief
- **Result** (market response) -- The belief echoed back by customers
- **Vital 3** -- Belief + Proof produced by the builder; Result returned by the market
- **Core Belief** -- "I believe that [Hidden Truth about the Broken Thing], and therefore I must [action the Legacy demands]"

**Note on local terminology.** Module 02's execution prompt uses Belief/Evidence/Opening as a local research lens for decoding competitor artifacts. That lens is distinct from the framework's Vital 3 above, which describes builder+market dynamics. See `02-Philosophy.md` > "Connection to the Vital 3".
